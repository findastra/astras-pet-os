## 1. Affective computing research: who leads it, how good is facial emotion recognition, and what are the scientific and legal limits?

### Takeaway
The field is active (MIT's Picard, Cambridge's Gunes, the ABAW competition series, LAION's open EmoNet models from 2025), but its basic premise is under heavy scientific attack: a face configuration does not reliably reveal a specific emotion (Barrett et al., 2019). Big vendors have pulled back (Microsoft retired Azure emotion inference in 2022), and since 2 February 2025 the EU AI Act bans emotion recognition from biometric data in workplaces and schools, while allowing it elsewhere with transparency duties.

### Cited Findings

**Leading people and labs**
- Rosalind W. Picard is founder and director of the Affective Computing research group at the MIT Media Lab and Grover M. Hermann Professor in Health Sciences and Technology; her bio says she received the 2026 IEEE Medal for Innovations in Healthcare Technology for pioneering wearable affective computing for health and wellbeing — [MIT Media Lab bio](https://www.media.mit.edu/people/bio_picard.html); research list at [web.media.mit.edu/~picard/research.php](https://web.media.mit.edu/~picard/research.php)
- Recent Picard-group work is health-focused rather than games: a Nature Medicine study with alum Matt Groh on AI-assisted dermatology diagnosis across skin tones; collaborations with Dentsu (smartphone/device data) and NEC (about 250,000 hours of data on chronic stress in workers). Dates were not shown in the snippets — [Affective Computing group updates](https://www-prod.media.mit.edu/groups/affective-computing/updates/); [Picard updates](https://www.media.mit.edu/people/picard/updates)
- Hatice Gunes, Professor of Affective Intelligence and Robotics at Cambridge, leads the Affective Intelligence and Robotics (AFAR) Lab. She is a former President of the Association for the Advancement of Affective Computing (AAAC, which runs the ACII conference) and Associate Director of the Centre for Human-Inspired AI. Her 2025 work centres on adaptive robotic wellbeing coaches (IEEE RAS lecture, Feb 2025) — [Gunes homepage](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~hg410); [AFAR Lab people](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~hg410/people.html); [IEEE WIE event](https://wie.ieee.org/event/ras-lecture-social-alignment-in-robotics-promoting-human-wellbeing-through-adaptive-robotic-coaches-prof-hatice-gunes/)
- USC Institute for Creative Technologies (older, 2013–2014): SimSensei, a virtual human interviewer ("Ellie"), used the MultiSense perception layer to track facial expressions, eye gaze, posture and voice in real time from a camera and microphone, and changed Ellie's behaviour in response (smiling at positive displays, nodding during pauses). Louis-Philippe Morency (MultiComp Lab), Skip Rizzo and Jonathan Gratch (Director of Virtual Human Research) led it. A 2013 study found people disclosed more when they believed no human was operating the agent — [Army.mil article](https://www.army.mil/article/147822/Researchers_create_virtual_humans); [ICT press release](https://ict.usc.edu/news/press-releases/ict-virtual-human-health-prototype-named-top-ten-digital-innovation-by-netexplo-forum/)
- ABAW (Affective Behavior Analysis in-the-wild), chaired by Dimitrios Kollias with Stefanos Zafeiriou, Irene Kotsia and Panagiotis Tzirakis: 8th edition at CVPR 2025 (12 June 2025) with challenges in valence-arousal estimation, expression recognition, action-unit detection, emotional mimicry intensity (using a Hume-provided dataset) and ambivalence/hesitancy recognition; a 9th edition ran at ICCV 2025 — [CVPR 2025 workshop page](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/virtual/2025/workshop/32345); [CfP mailing list](https://lists.uni-bielefeld.de/mailman2/unibi/public/iva-list/2025/002108.html); [ICCV 2025 workshop page](https://iccv.thecvf.com/virtual/2025/workshop/2805)
- LAION with Intel released EmoNet in 2025: open face and voice emotion tools. EmoNet-Face uses a 40-category emotion taxonomy and fully synthetic face images (to avoid privacy problems and widen demographic diversity); the models are "Empathic Insight-Face" Small/Large on Hugging Face, under Creative Commons (models) and Apache 2.0 (code). LAION reports a higher correlation with human expert ratings than Gemini 2.5 Pro or Hume AI's API (vendor-reported) — [LAION blog "Do They See What We See?"](https://laion.ai/blog/do-they-see-what-we-see/); [Hugging Face collection](https://huggingface.co/collections/laion/emonet); [arXiv 2505.20033](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20033v1); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/laion-and-intel-introduce-tools-that-help-ai-gauge-the-intensity-of-40-distinct-emotions/)
- Emteq Labs (UK) builds facial-muscle sensing for headsets and glasses: emteqPRO (2022) put facial EMG, heart rate, skin impedance and motion sensors into VR headsets, with a Unity SDK and cloud arousal/valence values (older). A May 2025 Bristol seminar describes its newer OCOsense wearable that combines facial-muscle activity, head movement and an onboard camera — [Frontiers in VR paper (2022)](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frvir.2022.781218/full); [Bristol Vision Institute seminar 2024–25](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/vision-institute/events/seminar-series/seminar-speakers/2024-25/outputurl-761494-en.html); [Road to VR (older)](https://roadtovr.com/emteq-aims-to-humanize-vr-by-capturing-your-facial-expressions/)

**Scientific criticism**
- Barrett, Adolphs, Marsella, Martinez and Pollak, "Emotional Expressions Reconsidered: Challenges to Inferring Emotion From Human Facial Movements", *Psychological Science in the Public Interest* 20(1):1–68, 2019, doi 10.1177/1529100619832930 (older but still the key reference). It reviewed six emotion categories and concluded that a facial configuration such as a scowl often communicates something other than an emotion. It proposed criteria (reliability, specificity, and others) that would be needed before inferring emotion from a face, and suggested neutral terms such as "facial configuration" — [APS publication page](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/emotional-expressions-reconsidered-challenges-to-inferring-emotion-from-human-facial-movements.html); [PMC author manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6640856/)
- Barrett: the evidence "never suggested that facial expressions are universal", despite claims by some scientists and many companies; studies in remote cultures found no facial configuration specific to a given emotion — [Northeastern News, 2019](https://news.northeastern.edu/2019/07/19/northeastern-university-professor-says-we-cant-gauge-emotions-from-facial-expressions-alone/); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/500592)
- Industry response (older): in June 2022 Microsoft, under Responsible AI Standard v2 (Natasha Crampton), retired Azure Face capabilities that infer emotional states and attributes such as gender, age, smile, facial hair, hair and makeup, citing the lack of scientific consensus on defining emotions. Existing customers had one year before losing access — [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2022/06/21/microsoft-restricts-access-controversial-ai-facial-recognition-technology/); [Azure update](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-in/updates/facelimitedaccess/)
- LAION itself notes open problems with culturally specific and mixed emotional states — [heise](https://heise.de/-10454055)

**Legal limits (EU AI Act)**
- Article 5(1)(f) bans AI systems that infer emotions from biometric data in workplaces and education institutions, from 2 February 2025, except for medical or safety reasons. The Commission's February 2025 guidelines say the ban also covers inferring *intentions* — [Future of Privacy Forum](https://fpf.org/blog/red-lines-under-eu-ai-act-unpacking-the-prohibition-of-emotion-recognition-in-the-workplace-and-education-institutions/); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/eu-commission-issues-guidelines-on-prohibited-ai-practices-part-ii)
- Per the guidelines (as summarised by Lewis Silkin): detecting whether someone is *smiling* is not emotion recognition, and neither is detecting fatigue to prevent accidents. Workplace burnout or depression detection remains banned (the wellbeing use is not exempt). Emotion recognition outside workplaces and schools (for example, a call centre tracking *customers'* emotions) is not banned, but is generally high-risk and has transparency duties under Article 50(3) — [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/02/17/understanding-the-eu-ai-acts-prohibited-practices-key-workplace-and-advertising-102k011)
- The guidelines are non-binding, and member states may adopt stricter rules — [Freshfields](https://technologyquotient.freshfields.com/post/102kfql/eu-ai-act-unpacked-25-european-commission-releases-critical-ai-act-implementati). One commentator argues the guidelines widen permitted uses (for example, training) beyond the Act's text — [Technology's Legal Edge](https://www.technologyslegaledge.com/?p=7255)

### Inferences
- For a hobbyist using her own face data at home, the Article 5 ban (workplace/education) does not appear to apply. A pet that labels a user's emotions and is *distributed* to others may fall under Article 50(3) transparency duties. This is an inference, not legal advice.
- Barrett's critique supports driving a pet's mood from *raw signals* (for example, Quest Pro blendshape weights such as smile or brow-lower) treated as "facial configurations", rather than asserting "the user is sad". The EU guidelines draw a similar line: smile detection is not emotion recognition.
- Open, local models (LAION EmoNet-Face) are now a realistic alternative to closed APIs for a hobbyist pipeline.

### Gaps
- Did not find a 2024–2026 independent benchmark comparing commercial facial emotion APIs.
- Did not confirm Jonathan Gratch's current (2024–2026) projects; the USC ICT material found is from 2013–2015.
- Emteq OCOsense's commercial status (price, shipping) is unverified.
- Could not open the full Barrett paper to quote its "reverse inference" wording.

## 2. Which AI character and companion platforms actually use the user's face or expression as input?

### Takeaway
Few do. Most "emotional" character platforms (Hume EVI, Inworld, NVIDIA ACE's Audio2Emotion) read *voice*, not the face. Those that use a camera on the user: Hume's new limited-release Expression Measurement API (October 2026; its old one shut down in June 2026), Tavus Raven (perception of the user's face in video calls), Razer Project AVA (a desk companion with a camera and eye tracking), Replika video calls, and NVIDIA ACE tech demos that use GPT-4o vision. Convai's "vision" sees the game scene, not your webcam. Soul Machines went into receivership in February 2026 and was bought by AppDirect in September 2026.

## 3. Games that use the player's face, eyes or body signals, and academic emotion-adaptive game AI

### Takeaway
Shipped examples are few but well known: *Before Your Eyes* (webcam blink detection drives the story), *Nevermind* (heart rate plus Affectiva webcam emotion sensing adapts the horror), and PSVR2 eye-tracking titles where characters react to gaze or blinks. Academically, Georgios Yannakakis's group at the University of Malta defines the field ("Affective Game Computing", 2023) and in 2025 is testing whether LLMs can model player engagement.

### Cited Findings

**Commercial games**
- *Before Your Eyes* (GoodbyeWorld Games, published by Skybound Games; PC 8 April 2021, later Android, iOS and PS5) uses the webcam to detect real blinks, which move the story forward through memories and make choices. A mouse click can substitute. It grew from the 2014 prototype "Close Your", which won an IndieCade Developer's Choice award (older) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Before_Your_Eyes); [Game Informer review](https://www.gameinformer.com/review/before-your-eyes/before-your-eyes-review-an-emotional-eye-opening-experience); [Gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/games/movie.asp?GRU=24308)
- *Nevermind* (Flying Mollusk, creative director Erin Reynolds) is a "biofeedback-enhanced psychological thriller". It started with a heart-rate monitor, then added Affectiva's Unity plug-in to read facial expressions via webcam for signs of distress; when the player calms down, the game becomes easier. Later additions included Apple Watch support and eye tracking. Webcam biofeedback is unavailable in VR (older; most sources are developer or partner material) — [Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/342260); [Affectiva case study](https://www.affectiva.com/success-story/flying-mollusk/); [Reynolds guest blog](https://blog.affectiva.com/finding-the-essence-of-fear-in-nevermind); [iMotions story](https://imotions.com/customer-stories/facing-your-fears-how-nevermind-uses-emotion-ai-to-master-your-nerves/)

It frames the field as the "affective loop" of elicitation, sensing, detection and adaptation, and reviews affect data-collection methods — [arXiv 2309.14104](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.14104); [UM repository](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/handle/123456789/146287)

- "Facial Emotion Recognition in VR Games" (arXiv 2312.06925, Dec 2023) reviews webcam facial-expression approaches to flow detection (a reported 78% accuracy) and difficulty adjustment — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.06925)
- Older: a Tilburg 2019 paper on modelling and adjusting game difficulty from facial expressions — [Tilburg University](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/modeling-and-adjusting-in-game-difficulty-based-on-facial-express/); BeFaced (FDG 2014 demo) used facial expressions as input for dynamic difficulty — [UTS repository](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/32766/1/fdg2014_demo_12.pdf)

### Inferences
- Shipped "face-reading" games mostly use *robust low-level signals* (blinks, gaze, heart rate) rather than emotion categories. That fits Barrett's critique and suggests a pet should react to blinks, gaze and smiles rather than inferred "sadness".
- The Malta group's move toward LLM-based engagement modelling (2025) parallels Astra's LLM-plus-mood pets; Yannakakis and Melhart are natural academic contacts.

- Did not find a 2024–2026 update to the Yannakakis survey. A 2025 "affect modelling via privileged information" paper (Wu, Barthet, Melhart, Yannakakis) was mentioned but its arXiv ID was not verified.
- *Nevermind*'s current status (updates or a sequel after 2020) is unknown.

## 4. AI that imitates or learns a specific person, and how identifiable VR face and motion data is

### Takeaway
"Digital twins" of real people now exist at two levels: *behavioural/attitudinal* (Stanford's 1,000-person interview agents; startups like Delphi that clone a person's knowledge and voice) and *nonverbal* (Meta FAIR's 2025 Seamless Interaction models, which generate a listener's facial reactions conditioned on the other person's face). VR tracking data is a strong biometric: Vivek Nair's Berkeley work identified 50,000+ users from head and hand motion alone, and 2024 work combines eye tracking with motion.

### Cited Findings

**Learning a specific person**
- Joon Sung Park et al. (Stanford), "Generative Agent Simulations of 1,000 People" (arXiv 2411.10109, Nov 2024): agents built from two-hour AI-conducted interviews with 1,052 people match their General Social Survey answers 85% as often as the people match their own answers two weeks later. Interview-based agents reduced accuracy gaps across racial and ideological groups compared with demographic-only agents — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109v1); [Stanford HAI policy brief](https://hai.stanford.edu/policy/simulating-human-behavior-with-ai-agents)
- Meta FAIR, "Seamless Interaction: Dyadic Audiovisual Motion Modeling and Large-Scale Dataset" (27 June 2025): 4,000+ hours of in-person conversations from 4,000+ participants, plus models that generate facial expressions and gestures from two-person speech (or LLM output). Visual variants take the *other person's* visual behaviour as input, learning mirrored smiles and joint gaze, with controllable emotional expressivity. Outputs can drive 2D video or 3D Codec Avatars. The dataset and code are public — [Meta research page](https://ai.meta.com/research/seamless-interaction/); [Meta blog](https://ai.meta.com/blog/seamless-interaction-natural-conversational-dynamics/); [arXiv 2506.22554](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.22554v2)
- Delphi (Dara Ladjevardian and Sam Spelsberg) sells "digital clones" of people via text, voice and video. It raised $16M on 24 June 2025 (CB Insights; Sequoia named as lead in one headline, unverified). Its origin was a model trained on Ladjevardian's late grandfather's book. It removed an unauthorised clone of Peter Attia after a takedown request (older VentureBeat piece) — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/delphi-2); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/you-can-now-make-an-ai-clone-of-yourself-or-anyone-else-living-or-dead-with-delphi)
- Tavus (section 2) also builds real-time video replicas of real people and pairs them with Raven perception of the user — [Tavus blog](https://tavus.io/blog/how-tavus-brings-human-computing-to-life)

**Privacy and identifiability**
- Vivek Nair (UC Berkeley, with Dawn Song; co-authors include Louis Rosenberg and Justus Mattern of Unanimous AI), "Unique Identification of 50,000+ Virtual Reality Users from Head & Hand Motion Data" (USENIX Security 2023; Beat Saber data; older). Users were identifiable from minutes of head and hand movement. A companion lab study, "Exploring the Unprecedented Privacy Risks of the Metaverse" (50 participants), went further. Nair's defences include "MetaGuard", an incognito mode for VR — [Berkeley EECS news](https://eecs.berkeley.edu/news/eecs-researchers-explore-unprecedented-privacy-risks-vr/); [The Register](https://theregister.com/2023/02/18/vr_telemetry_identity); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/new-research-suggests-that-privacy-in-the-metaverse-might-be-impossible)
- The Berkeley graphics lab lists Nair's 2024 follow-ups: how duration and delay affect identifiability of VR motion, motion re-identification under data degradation, "Truth in Motion" risks, and a real-time anonymisation method for VR motion — [Berkeley Graphics papers](https://graphics.berkeley.edu/papers/)
- "Exploring the Uncoordinated Privacy Protections of Eye Tracking and VR Motion Data for Unauthorized User Identification" (arXiv 2411.12766, Nov 2024) studies an adversary combining motion and eye-tracking streams, naming the Quest Pro among eye-tracked headsets — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.12766)
- Meta says Quest Pro "Natural Facial Expressions" processes raw images only on the headset, sends only abstracted expression data, and does not use it to identify you (company claim). Wired/Tom's Guide note that insights derived from eye-tracking images may be stored on Meta servers. Mozilla's *Privacy Not Included* rates Quest Pro poorly — [Road to VR](https://www.roadtovr.com/oculus-quest-camera-privacy-rift-s-facebook/); [Tom's Guide](https://tomsguide.com/news/new-meta-quest-pro-report-raises-serious-privacy-concerns-heres-why); [Mozilla PNI](https://www.mozillafoundation.org/en/privacynotincluded/meta-quest-pro/); [OECD AI incident entry](https://oecd.ai/en/incidents/2022-10-11-4dda)
- Background survey: "Eye-tracked Virtual Reality: A Comprehensive Survey on Methods and Privacy Challenges" (arXiv 2305.14080) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.14080v3)

### Inferences
- Astra's logged Quest Pro blendshape and eye streams are likely as identifying as the motion data in Nair's work. Anything she publishes (datasets, pet "memories" of her face) should be treated as biometric. This is an inference: no paper found tests *face-blendshape* re-identification specifically.
- The Seamless Interaction models are the closest public research to "a character whose face reacts to your face". Meta released them as open models, so a hobbyist could in principle feed them OSC-derived expression data. Whether they accept blendshape input directly is untested.

### Gaps
- Found no peer-reviewed paper specifically on re-identifying users from VR *facial-expression* (blendshape) data.
- Found no source on *behaviour cloning* of a specific person from their own VR tracking logs (for example, training an avatar to move or emote like its owner). Meta Codec Avatars are covered by another researcher.
- Did not find Nair's 2025–2026 work or current affiliation.

## 5. Hobbyists and indie developers combining LLMs with face tracking, webcams or VRChat

### Takeaway
There is an active open-source scene of LLM "AI companions" with camera vision and desktop-pet modes (Open-LLM-VTuber, Project AIRI), plus VRChat-specific glue (an MCP-to-OSC bridge for Claude, uezo's aiavatarkit driving VRChat facial expressions over OSC, and a Booth-sold Udon "AI NPC Kit"). No project was found that feeds a user's VRCFaceTracking data into an LLM character's mood. That combination appears to be an open niche.

### Cited Findings
- **Open-LLM-VTuber** (GitHub org Open-LLM-VTuber; MIT code): a voice-interactive Live2D AI companion that runs fully offline. It supports camera, screen recording and screenshots so "your AI companion [can] see you and your screen", has a transparent *desktop pet mode*, supports Ollama, OpenAI-compatible APIs, Claude and Gemini, and runs on Windows, macOS and Linux. A v2.0 rewrite is in planning — [GitHub](https://github.com/Open-LLM-VTuber/Open-LLM-VTuber); [docs](https://docs.llmvtuber.com/en/docs/intro/)
- **Project AIRI** (moeru-ai): an open-source "soul container" aiming to re-create Neuro-sama. It has Live2D/VRM support, desktop (Electron "Tamagotchi") and web builds, many LLM providers including Claude, and game-playing sub-projects (Minecraft, Factorio, Dome Keeper, Balatro). There were about 35k stars at AlternativeTo's snapshot, and the latest devlog seen was dated 2026-02-16 — [GitHub moeru-ai/airi](https://github.com/moeru-ai/airi); [proj-airi org](https://github.com/proj-airi); [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/project-airi/about)
- **kimjammer/Neuro**: a Neuro-sama recreation originally built in 7 days (mentioned in Project AIRI search results; repo not opened) — URL inferred from the repo name: [github.com/kimjammer/Neuro](https://github.com/kimjammer/Neuro)
- **Krekun/vrchat-mcp-osc**: an MCP server that lets Claude Desktop control a VRChat avatar over OSC (set avatar parameters and emotes, move, look, jump, send chatbox messages; default ports 9000/9001) — [mcpservers.org listing](https://mcpservers.org/servers/Krekun/vrchat-mcp-osc)
- **uezo/aiavatarkit**: a Python SDK for conversational avatars (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Dify; VOICEVOX TTS) with "real-time facial expression control for VRChat using Avatar OSC"; MIT — [SourcePulse summary](https://www.sourcepulse.org/projects/1833526)
- **AI NPC Kit (Booth)**: a paid VRChat Udon world kit. Udon cannot build URLs at runtime, so it offers LLM-generated reply *choices*, routed through the creator's own Cloudflare Worker to Claude (default) or an OpenAI-compatible API (seller's claims) — [Booth listing](https://booth.pm/ja/items/8822858)
- Academic: "Building LLM-based AI Agents in Social Virtual Reality" deployed a GPT-4 agent as an NPC *inside VRChat* that answers players with context-relevant speech plus matching facial expressions and gestures — [Nweon paper summary](https://paper.nweon.com/?p=15770)
- Meta is adding LLM (Llama 4) NPCs to Horizon Worlds that talk and react in real time — [Road to VR](https://roadtovr.com/meta-horizon-worlds-ai-npcs-update-llama-4/); [Meta developer blog](https://developers.meta.com/vr/blog/worlds/environment-generation-embodied-conversational-llm-npcs-genai-tools/)
- Webcam-emotion-to-LLM hobby pieces: **EmoChat** (Dani-zm) captures webcam frames each second, classifies emotion locally (OpenCV plus scikit-learn) and sends only emotion *labels*, not images, to Gemini for empathetic replies — [EmoChat docs](https://www.mintlify.com/Dani-zm/emotion-recognition-ai/quickstart). **EmotionTracker** (DeepPythonist) is a local DeepFace real-time emotion desktop app — [GitHub](https://github.com/DeepPythonist/EmotionTracker)
- Academic analogue to an AI desktop pet: "An LLM-Based Interaction System with Multimodal Emotion" for intelligent electronic pets (2025), where multimodal sensors feed an LLM decision layer that produces personalised pet feedback (code not found) — [SciTePress PDF](https://www.scitepress.org/publishedPapers/2025/143607/pdf/index.html)
- Older but relevant: a ChatGPT-powered *Skyrim VR* mod showed LLM NPCs in VR — [Road to VR](https://www.roadtovr.com/chatgpt-skyrim-vr-ai-mod/)

### Inferences
- Astra's stack (Quest Pro → VRCFaceTracking → OSC) already produces exactly the kind of signal these projects lack. Combining it with an MCP/OSC bridge (Krekun) or aiavatarkit-style OSC output would give a two-way loop: *her* face in, *pet* expression out.
- The privacy-preserving pattern used by EmoChat (send labels or blendshape numbers, never images, to the LLM) suits a desktop pet and the identifiability concerns in section 4.
- The closest "people doing similar work" communities to approach are the Open-LLM-VTuber and Project AIRI contributor communities, uezo (aiavatarkit), and the Malta games-AI group.

### Gaps
- Found no public project that feeds VRCFaceTracking or Quest Pro face-tracking data into an LLM character's mood or memory.
- Did not open the aiavatarkit, vrchat-mcp-osc or kimjammer/Neuro GitHub repos directly; maintenance status is unverified.
- No data on how many hobbyist LLM NPCs exist in VRChat.
- Could not verify whether Open-LLM-VTuber's camera input does any *expression* analysis or just passes frames to a vision LLM.
