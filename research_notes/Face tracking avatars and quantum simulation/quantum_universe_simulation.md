## Q1. Quantum simulation of high-energy physics: lattice gauge theories, the Schwinger model, string breaking, hadron dynamics (who, what, how big)

### Takeaway
Between 2024 and 2026 the field went from toy circuits of a few qubits to simulations of 1+1D quantum electrodynamics (QED, the Schwinger model) on more than 100 qubits (IBM, University of Washington InQubator), and to the first 2+1D gauge-theory "string breaking" experiments on about 45 superconducting qubits (Google) and on Rydberg-atom arrays (Innsbruck/Harvard/QuEra), both published in Nature in June 2025. A wave of 2026 preprints has since moved to non-Abelian theories (SU(2), the kind of theory behind the strong force) and to glueballs and hadron scattering. All of these are still simplified models (low dimension, small lattices, truncated fields). None is a demonstrated "quantum advantage" over classical computers for particle physics, and full 3+1D QCD (the theory of quarks and gluons) is not yet close.

### Cited Findings
**Older foundations (before 2023, for context):**
- Jordan, Lee and Preskill (2012) gave the first quantum algorithm with polynomial cost for computing scattering in a quantum field theory (φ⁴ theory). This is the theoretical starting point the field cites. **[prior knowledge, not re-verified]** — [arXiv:1111.3633, Science 336, 1130 (2012)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.3633)
- In 2016, Innsbruck ran the first digital quantum simulation of the Schwinger model (1+1D QED), with pair creation, on 4 trapped-ion qubits (Martinez et al., Nature 534, 516). **[prior knowledge, not re-verified]** — [Nature 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature18318)
- Klco, Savage and co-authors (2018) computed Schwinger-model dynamics with a hybrid quantum-classical method on IBM cloud devices. This was early InQubator-lineage work. **[prior knowledge, not re-verified]** — [arXiv:1803.03326](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.03326)

**Roadmaps that define the field:**
- Bauer, Davoudi and about 30 co-authors, "Quantum Simulation for High-Energy Physics" (PRX Quantum 4, 027001, May 2023; arXiv:2204.03381). It lays out the promises, requirements and challenges "for the next decade and beyond." It says the first step is to find problems in collider physics, neutrino physics, cosmology and quantum gravity that classical methods cannot solve but quantum simulators might. — [APS](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PRXQuantum.4.027001); [OSTI](https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/1972393)
- Di Meglio et al., "Quantum Computing for High-Energy Physics: State of the Art and Challenges" (PRX Quantum 5, 037001, 2024; arXiv:2307.03236). It summarizes the QC4HEP working group led by CERN, DESY and IBM. It says the qubit counts and circuit depths these simulations need are "largely beyond" current noisy devices, and it names the sign problem (classical sampling of real-time or high-density fermion systems blows up exponentially) as the core reason quantum methods are wanted. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.03236); [DOI](https://dx.doi.org/10.1103/PRXQuantum.5.037001)
- TASI/CERN/KITP lecture notes "Toward Quantum Computing Gauge Theories of Nature" (arXiv:2507.15840, 2025) are a current teaching reference. I did not confirm the author. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.15840)

- The "size" frontier as of late 2026: about 100–112 qubits for 1+1D U(1), about 45 qubits for 2+1D Z₂ on superconducting hardware, roughly 100 qubits on trapped-ion machines, and 2026 non-Abelian work on qudit platforms. The physics is still made of "minimal" theories (Z₂, U(1), truncated SU(2)), far from QCD with SU(3) in 3+1D.
- The pattern is that each step either adds a dimension (1+1D to 2+1D), moves from Abelian to non-Abelian, or goes from static to scattering. 2025–2026 delivered all three in small form, which suggests the next milestone will be small 2+1D non-Abelian scattering with error detection or early error correction.

## Q2. Analog and digital simulations of cosmology and gravity: expanding universes, particle creation, Hawking radiation, the "wormhole," holography

### Takeaway
Real laboratory "universes" exist, but they are analogs. Sound waves in ultracold gases obey the same equations as quantum fields in curved or expanding spacetime, so labs can watch "particle creation" (phonons) during a simulated expansion (Heidelberg, Oberthaler group) and Hawking-like emission at analog horizons (Bose–Einstein condensates, and a 10-qubit superconducting chip). They test the mathematics of quantum fields in curved spacetime, not gravity itself. The 2022 Google Sycamore "wormhole" was 9 qubits and 164 two-qubit gates. Independent physicists disputed that it showed wormhole physics, and it is widely treated as an overhyped toy holography demonstration.

### Cited Findings
**Expanding-universe and curved-spacetime analogs (Heidelberg, Markus Oberthaler):**
- Viermann et al., "Quantum field simulator for dynamics in curved spacetime," Nature 611, 260 (2022). The Heidelberg group used a 2D Bose–Einstein condensate as a "curved spacetime in the lab." — [Heidelberg University press release](https://www.uni-heidelberg.de/en/newsroom/curved-spacetime-in-the-lab); citation confirmed via reference lists, e.g. [arXiv:2603.16095](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.16095)
- Method: ramp a magnetic field near a Feshbach resonance, which changes the strength of interactions between atoms. The condensate stays the same physical size but behaves as if space were expanding. Density fluctuations play the role of quantum fields, and the "created particles" are phonons. — [Physics Today, "The early universe in a quantum gas"](https://physicstoday.aip.org/news/the-early-universe-in-a-quantum-gas)
- Limit: the analog expands by a factor of less than 3, "a far cry from the 10^26" assumed by inflation theory. — [Physics Today](https://pubs.aip.org/physicstoday/online/41858/The-early-universe-in-a-quantum-gas)
- Sparn et al. (2024–2025) read particle production in a 2D condensate with tunable interactions as a scattering problem, linking resonant forward scattering and Bragg reflection to cosmological counterparts. — [PubMed 39879056, "Experimental Particle Production in Time-Dependent Spacetimes: A One-Dimensional Scattering Problem"](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39879056/)
- 2026 theory follow-up: "Efimovian Phonon Production for an Analog Coasting Universe in Bose–Einstein Condensates" (arXiv:2603.16095). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.16095)
- Older precedents: Eckel et al., "A rapidly expanding Bose–Einstein condensate: an expanding universe in the lab" (arXiv:1710.05800, published 2018), and inflation analogs in toroidal condensates (arXiv:2009.05611). — [1710.05800](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.05800); [2009.05611](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.05611)
- A 2025 review of ultracold atoms as simulators of relativistic phenomena. — [arXiv:2501.03785](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.03785)

**Early-universe phase transitions (false vacuum decay):**
- Vodeb, Desaules, Hallam et al. (corresponding author Zlatko Papić, Leeds), "Stirring the false vacuum via interacting quantized bubbles on a 5,564-qubit quantum annealer," Nature Physics 21, 386–392 (2025). They watched quantized "bubbles" of true vacuum form and interact in real time on a **D-Wave annealer with 5,564 superconducting flux qubits**. The model was 1D, and the authors think 3D versions may be possible on the same machine. The cosmology link (how transitions after the Big Bang might have happened) is interpretive, not a test of whether our universe is in a false vacuum. — [Leeds news](https://www.leeds.ac.uk/news/article/5724/quantum-machine-offers-peek-into-dance-of-cosmic-bubbles); [White Rose eprint](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/218849/); [ScienceDaily, Feb 2025](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2025/02/250204132418.htm)

**Hawking radiation analogs:**
- BAQIS and the Institute of Physics (Chinese Academy of Sciences), "Quantum simulation of Hawking radiation and curved spacetime with a superconducting on-chip black hole," Nature Communications (2023). It used a chain of **10 transmon qubits with 9 tunable couplers** to model a lattice black hole. Quasi-particles tunnel out through the "horizon" with a probability that matches a blackbody (thermal) spectrum. — [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-39064-6); [arXiv:2111.11092](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.11092); [BAQIS news](http://en.baqis.ac.cn/news/detail/?cid=1714)
- Bose–Einstein condensates have the best track record for Hawking-like radiation. The Technion group (Jeff Steinhauer) measured density correlations consistent with entangled Hawking pairs, though one review notes that correlation measurements alone do not prove entanglement without extra assumptions. An early example is the 2014 "black hole laser" with self-amplifying Hawking radiation. — [arXiv:1409.6550](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.6550); review context in [arXiv:2303.00814, "Analogue Quantum Simulation: A New Instrument for Scientific Understanding"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.00814)
- Optical analogs have reproduced horizons and stimulated emission but have not targeted spontaneous Hawking emission. — [Nature Communications 2023 (background section)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10241825/)
- My search found no notable new 2025–2026 Hawking-analog experiment. That may be a gap in the search rather than a gap in the field.

**The 2022 Google Sycamore "wormhole" and the criticism:**
- Jafferis, Zlokapa, Lykken, Kolchmeyer, Davis, Lauk, Neven and Spiropulu, "Traversable wormhole dynamics on a quantum processor," Nature 612, 51–55 (December 2022). They ran a "sparsified" SYK model (a toy model linked to black holes by holographic duality) on a **9-qubit circuit with 164 two-qubit gates** on Google Sycamore. They claimed "perfect size winding" and other wormhole-like signatures, such as a Shapiro-like time delay. — [IDEAS/RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v612y2022i7938d10.1038_s41586-022-05424-3.html); [Caltech INQNET page](https://inqnet.caltech.edu/wormhole2022/); [CaltechAUTHORS](https://resolver.caltech.edu/CaltechAUTHORS:20221117-173105698)

In their view, good size winding appears only for the two fermions actually used, and it may be an artifact of sparsification and small size rather than gravity-like physics. Jafferis et al. replied with a revised Hamiltonian ("Comment on 'Comment on...'"). — [arXiv:2308.00697, "Debating the Reliability and Robustness of the Learned Hamiltonian in the Traversable Wormhole Experiment"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.00697); the Kobrin et al. comment itself is arXiv:2302.07897 **[prior knowledge, not re-verified]** — [arXiv:2302.07897](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07897)
- Quanta Magazine added an update to its video saying an independent team presented evidence that the experiment "did not create any wormholes, holographic or otherwise." Peter Woit, an openly critical source, called the publicity a "stunt." — [Woit, "Not Quite What Happened"](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~woit/wordpress/?p=13425); [Woit, "Zombie Wormhole Publicity Stunt"](https://math.columbia.edu/~woit/wordpress/?p=14052)
- Spiropulu's defence: "We observed features of the coupled systems consistent with traversable wormhole teleportation." — quoted via [arXiv:2308.00697](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.00697)

**"It from Qubit" and holography:**
- The Simons Collaboration "It From Qubit: Quantum Fields, Gravity and Information" brings quantum-information theorists together with physicists working on quantum gravity and quantum field theory (AdS/CFT holography, multipartite entanglement). ### Inferences
- "Simulating the early universe" today means one carefully chosen effect (particle creation during expansion, bubble nucleation, or horizon emission) in a dimensionally reduced analog (1D or 2D) with stand-in fields (phonons, spins). Expansion factors are about 3, not 10^26, and gravity is not dynamical. These experiments test quantum field theory in a fixed curved background, not quantum gravity.
- The wormhole episode is a useful lesson for Astra: a small-qubit experiment can be faithful to a highly simplified (and possibly classically easy) model while its connection to real gravity remains a matter of interpretation. Check what is actually being simulated, not the headline.
- Quantum gravity itself (dynamical spacetime) has no direct quantum simulation. The holographic route (simulate a boundary quantum system such as SYK, then infer the gravity) is the main proposal, and it needs far larger and cleaner machines than 9 qubits.

## Q3. Fundamental limits: can a quantum computer inside the universe simulate the whole universe?

### Takeaway
No, not at full resolution. Seth Lloyd's bound says the observable universe could have performed at most about 10^120 operations on about 10^90 bits (about 10^120 if gravitational degrees of freedom are counted). Any exact simulation of the universe would need at least those resources, and they cannot fit inside a subsystem of the universe. Franco Vazza's 2025 Frontiers in Physics paper adds energy costs and concludes that even a low-resolution simulation of Earth alone is physically implausible for a simulator with our physics. Formal computer-science work (Wolpert) shows that some facts can never be known by any inference device inside the universe, but also that self-simulation is not ruled out by logic alone. The binding limits are physical (energy, entropy, holographic bounds), not logical.

### Cited Findings
- Lloyd, "Computational capacity of the universe" (arXiv:quant-ph/0110141; Phys. Rev. Lett. 2002): the universe "could have performed no more than 10^120 ops on 10^90 bits." The bit count is about 10^90 for matter and about 10^120 if gravitation is included. The operations bound comes from the Margolus–Levitin limit (maximum operation rate per unit energy) times the age of the universe. Lloyd frames these as *lower bounds* on the operations and bits needed to simulate the whole universe on a quantum computer. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0110141); [ar5iv HTML](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0110141); [Discover summary](https://www.discovermagazine.com/number-crunching-the-cosmos-14026)
- The same paper uses entropy bounds (number of bits ≤ S/(k_B ln 2)) and cites Bekenstein's result that the ratio of processing rate to input/output rate is at least 1/2π, with equality for black holes. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0110141)
- Vazza, "Astrophysical constraints on the simulation hypothesis for this Universe," Frontiers in Physics, published 17 April 2025 (DOI 10.3389/fphy.2025.1561873; arXiv:2504.08461). The argument is that information has a physical (energy) cost. It sizes three cases with holographic and Bekenstein-type information bounds: the full visible universe, Earth only, and a low-resolution Earth that is still consistent with the high-energy neutrinos we observe. The energy or power required is "either incompatible with physics or astronomically large" in all cases. The paper concludes that a universe with our physics simulating us is "simply impossible, regardless of technological advancements." — [Frontiers PDF](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physics/articles/10.3389/fphy.2025.1561873/pdf); [arXiv:2504.08461](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2504.08461)
- Press coverage of Vazza: the energy cost grows with each timestep, so after about 10^6 timesteps the energy needed would equal the rest-mass energy of the Milky Way or the potential energy of the most massive galaxy clusters. — [IFLScience](https://www.iflscience.com/new-study-rules-out-popular-version-of-the-simulation-hypothesis-79476)
- Vazza notes that a key assumption in Bekenstein's derivation, that the system's gravitational self-interaction can be ignored, matters for whole-universe cases. — [arXiv:2504.08461](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2504.08461)
- Critiques of Vazza: Lincoln Cannon argues that the energy assumptions overreach and that "impossible" is overstated. A "simulism" Substack argues the paper does not engage Bostrom's actual hypothesis. Commentators note the result does not rule out a parent universe with different physics. — [Lincoln Cannon](https://lincoln.metacannon.net/2025/05/vazza-overstates-constraints-on-simulation.html); [Simulism Substack](https://simulism.substack.com/p/physicists-keep-refuting-the-wrong)
- Wolpert, "Physical limits of inference" (2008 talk/paper): any "inference device" (something that observes, predicts or remembers) inside a universe has limits. In any such system, some quantities cannot be ascertained by any device inside it, regardless of the details of physical law. — [PIRSA talk](https://pirsa.org/08110000); [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/limits-on-human-comprehension)
- Wolpert, "What computer science has to say about the simulation hypothesis," Journal of Physics: Complexity (2025). Using Kleene's recursion theorem, it argues that if some universe can simulate ours, nothing *logically* prevents ours from simulating it back. Simulations need not degrade, and infinite chains are formally consistent. This is a logical result, not a physical-resources result. — [Mapping Ignorance, Jan 2026](https://mappingignorance.org/2026/01/06/simulation-hypothesis/); [Complexity Digest, Dec 2025](https://comdig.cssociety.org/2025/12/26/what-computer-science-has-to-say-about-the-simulation-hypothesis/)

### Inferences
- Why a part cannot fully simulate the whole: an exact simulation needs at least as many independent degrees of freedom as the thing simulated. A quantum computer built inside the universe has strictly fewer degrees of freedom (bits) than the universe, and it cannot exceed the holographic or Bekenstein entropy of its own region. So a full-resolution simulation of the whole universe by an internal machine is ruled out by counting, before engineering even starts. This is my synthesis of Lloyd and Vazza; neither phrase it as a theorem in the snippets I saw.
- Other limits that follow from standard physics (my inference; no specific source was fetched for these): (a) you cannot measure the universe's exact quantum state to load it as input (no-cloning; full state tomography needs exponentially many measurements); (b) chaotic dynamics amplify any error in the initial data, so long-time point-by-point prediction fails even with a perfect simulator; (c) what we can see is limited by the cosmological horizon.
- The useful framing for Astra: a quantum computer simulates *models* (Hamiltonians) of pieces of the universe at chosen resolution, never "the universe." Lloyd's numbers show the gap. 10^90–10^120 bits compares with roughly 10^2–10^3 physical qubits today, and Bauer-roadmap-scale targets are thousands to millions of logical qubits (see Q5).
- Vazza's result constrains *our-physics* simulators of *our* universe. Wolpert's result is purely logical. They do not contradict each other because they answer different questions.

### Gaps
- I could not read Vazza's full text (frontiersin.org was blocked), so the paper's specific bit counts and energy numbers for each case are not captured here. Only the press-reported 10^6-timestep figure is.
- I found no peer-reviewed rebuttal of Vazza in Frontiers in Physics, though a Substack claims one exists.
- I did not find a recent (2023–2026) formal paper proving "a subsystem cannot simulate the whole," beyond Lloyd, Wolpert and Vazza.

## Q4. What counts as "simulating a universe" realistically: toy universes, 1+1D field theory, digital physics, and the 2030s–2040s framing

### Takeaway
Researchers do not talk about simulating the universe. They talk about simulating specific Hamiltonians: lattice gauge theories (Schwinger model, Z₂, U(1), truncated SU(2) and SU(3)), scalar field theories, neutrino systems, and analog curved-spacetime field theories. Their goals are things classical computers cannot do because of the sign problem: real-time dynamics, collisions, hadronization, dense matter, and the early universe. "Universe as computation" ideas (Wheeler's "it from bit," Lloyd, the Wolfram Physics Project) are a separate, philosophical or speculative track that does not drive the experimental program.

### Cited Findings
- The Bauer et al. roadmap frames the realistic program as finding concrete HEP problems (collider physics, neutrinos, cosmology, quantum gravity) that classical methods cannot solve, over "the next decade and beyond." — [PRX Quantum 4, 027001](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PRXQuantum.4.027001)
- Di Meglio et al. (CERN/DESY/IBM QC4HEP) frame near-term work as benchmark applications, and give the sign problem (exponential cost of classical real-time and high-density fermion simulation) as the reason quantum methods matter. — [PRX Quantum 5, 037001](https://dx.doi.org/10.1103/PRXQuantum.5.037001)
- The DOE framing: Standard Model equations "in situations involving dynamics or high densities" are "too difficult to solve or simulate with the most powerful classical supercomputers," and scalable quantum circuits are "a promising way to eventually" simulate matter in extreme conditions. This is an aspiration, not a result. — [DOE](https://www.energy.gov/science/np/articles/nuclear-physicists-create-scalable-quantum-circuits-simulate-fundamental)
- Representative "toy universe" targets in 2025–2026 papers: Schwinger model with a dynamical axion (arXiv:2603.12194); a coordinate-space representation of scalar field theory (arXiv:2608.00670); continuum field theories from large-spin lattice models (arXiv:2412.15325); a universal framework for Yang–Mills theory (arXiv:2411.13161); qu8its (8-level qudits) for lattice QCD (arXiv:2403.14537); a QCD splitting-function algorithm (arXiv:2605.06789); decay rates at second order in perturbation theory (arXiv:2409.12236). — [2603.12194](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.12194); [2608.00670](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.00670); [2412.15325](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.15325); [2411.13161](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.13161); [2403.14537](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.14537); [2605.06789](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.06789); [2409.12236](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.12236)
- A 2025 review of gauge symmetry in quantum simulation (arXiv:2512.22932) and 2026 work on "Gauss law codes and vacuum codes from lattice gauge theories" (arXiv:2604.06087), which uses the gauge structure itself for error correction. — [2512.22932](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.22932); [2604.06087](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.06087)
- Feynman's 1981/82 "Simulating Physics with Computers" is still the founding statement that quantum systems are best simulated by quantum machines (2024 commentary). — [arXiv:2405.03366](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.03366)
- Philosophy-of-science framing: analog quantum simulators are described as "a new instrument for scientific understanding" that gives evidence about the model, not direct observation of the target system (for example a real black hole). — [arXiv:2303.00814](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.00814)
- Lloyd's computational view of the universe ("the universe as a quantum computer") gives the 10^120-operation figure. — [arXiv:quant-ph/0110141](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0110141)
- "It from Qubit" (Simons) is the institutional descendant of Wheeler's "it from bit," treating spacetime as possibly emerging from quantum entanglement. — [Simons Foundation](https://www.simonsfoundation.org/collaborations/it-from-qubit/)
- The Wolfram Physics Project (launched 2020) proposes that the universe emerges from hypergraph rewriting rules. It is a speculative digital-physics program outside mainstream physics and is not a quantum-computing simulation effort. **[prior knowledge, not re-verified]** — [wolframphysics.org](https://www.wolframphysics.org/)

### Inferences
- What Astra could realistically "simulate the universe" with later, in rising order of difficulty: (1) the 1+1D Schwinger model (pair creation, string breaking, hadron scattering), which today runs on about 100 qubits and is classically checkable; (2) 2+1D Z₂ and U(1) gauge theories (confinement, glueballs), which run today on about 45–100 qubits; (3) truncated SU(2) and SU(3) on small lattices; (4) analog cosmology toys (expanding BEC universes, false-vacuum bubbles), which are lab experiments rather than programs; (5) full 3+1D QCD dynamics, a fault-tolerant-era goal (see Q5). Steps 1–2 can already be done by hobbyists on cloud hardware or classical emulators at small sizes. That last point is my inference; other researchers cover access and cost.
- Digital-physics ideas (Wolfram, "it from bit") are worth reading for motivation, but no quantum-computing research group in these sources uses them as a working simulation target.

### Gaps
- I did not find a published, consensus 2030s–2040s milestone timeline for HEP quantum simulation. The roadmaps speak of "the next decade and beyond" without dates.
- I did not verify current Wolfram Physics Project status or claims (no search was run on it).

## Q5. When could fault-tolerant quantum computers do useful high-energy-physics or cosmology simulations beyond classical? (logical qubits, gates)

### Takeaway
Published resource estimates for the real target, 3+1D SU(3) lattice QCD dynamics, have fallen from absurd levels (Trotter-based Kan and Nam: about 10^40–10^55 T gates, reported only in garbled snippets) to about 10^27 T gates (qubitization), with 2025–2026 work cutting per-step costs further. The latest block-encoding estimate needs about 12·L³ + O(10³) logical qubits (about 12,000 for a 10³ lattice). Even at the optimistic end, meaningful QCD simulations need millions to tens of millions of logical-qubit-hours with very high T-gate throughput, which puts them after the first generation of fault-tolerant machines. Smaller targets (1+1D and 2+1D theories, truncated SU(2)) are the plausible first "beyond-classical" HEP results. No source gives a firm year.

### Cited Findings
- Kan and Nam's Trotter-based fault-tolerant analysis (arXiv:2107.12769) gave explicit T-gate counts for gauge theories in general dimension, with SU(3) costs a 2025 paper calls "prohibitively enormous." One garbled snippet cites about 10^40 (SU(2)) and about 10^55 (SU(3)); treat those exponents with caution. — characterized in [arXiv:2608.17115, "Block Encoding Non-Abelian Lattice Gauge Theory"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.17115) and [arXiv:2503.11888](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.11888)
- A qubitization-based follow-up cut that gate count to **1.7 × 10^27** T gates. — as cited in [arXiv:2503.11888, "Efficient Truncations of SU(N_c) Lattice Gauge Theory for Quantum Simulation"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.11888)
- Efficient truncations (arXiv:2503.11888, 2025): a (2,2,2) truncation brings the per-Trotter-step T count to O(10^10), and a (1,2,1) truncation to about 10^8. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.11888)
- "Exponential improvements in the simulation of lattice gauge theories using near-optimal techniques" (arXiv:2405.10416, 2024) gives post-Trotter (LCU and qubitization) resource estimates for U(1), SU(2) and SU(3), compared against Kan and Nam's estimates for a 2D U(1) heavy-ion case. I did not confirm the author list or the SU(3) totals. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.10416)
- Balaji et al., "Quantum circuits for SU(3) lattice gauge theory" (arXiv:2503.08866; published in a Phys. Rev. journal, DOI 10.1103/k8f6-yft8): explicit CX and T counts per plaquette and for small lattices, with a multi-controlled-X decomposition that roughly halves gate depth. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.08866); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/k8f6-yft8)
- "Block Encoding Non-Abelian Lattice Gauge Theory" (arXiv:2608.17115, August 2026): one call to the plaquette oracle costs about 10^5–10^6 T gates at the chosen truncations, and the logical qubit count is **Q ≈ 12L³ + O(10³)** for a full periodic L³ lattice. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.17115)
- For comparison (chemistry benchmarks quoted in search results): a 2017 FeMoco estimate needed about 111 logical qubits and about 10^14 T gates with Trotterization. 2D Fermi–Hubbard with plaquette Trotterization needs about 10^6 Toffoli gates and about 130 logical qubits. — [PostQuantum "Quantum Utility Ladder"](https://postquantum.com/quantum-utility-map/quantum-utility-ladder-fault-tolerant-algorithms/)
- Di Meglio et al. (2024): the qubit counts and depths needed are largely beyond near-term devices. — [arXiv:2307.03236](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.03236)
- 2026 work on spin-qubit applications lists NISQ-to-fault-tolerance benchmarks (arXiv:2609.07210). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.07210)
- An example of the scaling literature ("How to Build a Quantum Supercomputer: Scaling from Hundreds to Millions of Qubits," arXiv:2411.10406). Other researchers cover hardware roadmaps. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.10406v4)

### Inferences
- Arithmetic from the 12L³ formula: L = 10 needs about 13,000 logical qubits, L = 16 about 50,000, and L = 32 about 400,000. Lattice QCD practitioners typically want L ≥ 32 for physics results, so full-scale QCD dynamics implies hundreds of thousands of logical qubits. At 10^27 T gates and an assumed (optimistic) 10^6–10^9 logical T gates per second, run times would be 10^18–10^21 seconds, longer than the age of the universe (about 4×10^17 s). So the 10^27 figure is still not practical. Usable QCD needs further algorithmic reductions of many orders of magnitude, or smaller physics targets. This is my calculation from the cited figures; the T-rate assumptions are mine.
- Per-Trotter-step costs of about 10^8–10^10 T gates (aggressive truncation, lattice size unspecified) times about 10^2–10^4 steps gives 10^10–10^14 T gates. That is the same order as early chemistry (FeMoco) estimates, so small-lattice truncated SU(2) or SU(3) dynamics may become feasible in the same era as the first fault-tolerant chemistry applications. This is speculation built on the cited per-step numbers.
- Likely ordering of first "beyond-classical" HEP results: 2+1D gauge theories and large 1+1D scattering (possibly late 2020s to early 2030s on early error-corrected machines), then truncated non-Abelian dynamics (2030s), then real QCD observables (2040s or later). This is my synthesis; none of the fetched sources give dates.

### Gaps
- I did not confirm the exact Kan and Nam T-count exponents (the snippet was garbled), or which paper produced the 1.7×10^27 figure (likely arXiv:2405.10416, but not confirmed).
- I found no dedicated fault-tolerant resource estimate for cosmology simulations (inflation, reheating, false vacuum in 3+1D) or for holographic or SYK quantum-gravity simulations at a scientifically useful size.
- I found no authoritative dated forecast of when HEP quantum simulation will beat classical methods. Roadmaps avoid dates.
