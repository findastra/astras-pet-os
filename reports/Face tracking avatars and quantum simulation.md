# Track the face, turn the phase

Astra is not alone, but the exact thing she is building has no public owner yet. Dozens of named builders and labs work on pieces of her pipeline, from the volunteer maintainers of VRCFaceTracking and Project Babble and the developers of VTuber software to the avatar labs at Meta and KAIST and robotics groups at Columbia, Honda Research Institute Europe and in Japan, which drive robot faces with the same 0-to-1 headset blendshape values she already streams over OSC. Yet this research found **no public project that feeds VRCFaceTracking data into an AI character's mood, and none that drives a physical servo face from it**. VRChat made her data format mainstream on **28 October 2026** by shipping native face tracking built on VRCFT's Unified Expressions standard, so now is a good time to publish tools and join those communities. On the quantum side, Euler's formula e^(iθ) really is the grammar of quantum computing (every gate is an Euler-style rotation, and the Hadamard gate's cancellation is exactly e^(iπ) + 1 = 0), but it is the machine's language, not its engine, and it says nothing about which physics to simulate. Nobody simulates "our universe", because a computer inside the universe cannot hold the universe's **~10^90 to 10^120 bits**; researchers simulate "toy universes" instead, such as 1+1-dimensional electromagnetism on 112 IBM qubits and 2+1-dimensional string breaking on 45 Google qubits. Full 3+1-dimensional Standard Model physics needs **10^4 to 10^8 error-corrected logical qubits**, against the **200** IBM promises for 2029, which puts it decades away with no year anyone can defend. Her cost assumption is wrong in her favour: she can run real quantum hardware today for **$0**, simulate **about 31 qubits exactly on a 24 GB gaming GPU**, and will never need to buy a quantum computer, because the machines that matter are rented through the cloud. What holds her back is skill and a portfolio of work, not money.

## VRChat's October 2026 launch made Astra's face data the standard

The open-source stack Astra uses is maintained by a small group of people who can be named. **VRCFaceTracking** was created by and belongs to **benaclejames**. It is Apache-2.0 licensed and had 917 stars and 1,220 commits on 3 November 2026 ([GitHub](https://github.com/benaclejames/VRCFaceTracking)). Release notes credit hyblocker, RantiMess and kusomaigo, plus translators P4tr0ll, stlenx, nkotech and VirtualMarten ([VRCFT releases](https://github.com/benaclejames/VRCFaceTracking/releases)). Earlier releases credited dfgHiatus and regzo2 ([VRCFT v4.0.0](https://github.com/benaclejames/VRCFaceTracking/releases/tag/v4.0.0)). VRCFT now ships on Steam ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3329480/VRCFaceTracking/)), where average monthly players rose from 725 to 1,137 between January and April 2026 ([stmstat](https://stmstat.com/pt/app/3329480)). The VRCFaceTracking GitHub organisation hosts **VRCFTModuleExample**, the starting template for anyone writing a hardware module ([GitHub org](https://github.com/VRCFaceTracking)). A 2026 fork by **RealWhyKnot** (v2026.9.18.0, 18 September 2026) adds Windows, Linux and macOS builds and multi-module tracking, with contributions from Happyrobot33, lumeyisnotyou and yuki-maruyama ([release](https://github.com/RealWhyKnot/VRCFaceTracking/releases/tag/v2026.9.18.0)). Whether benaclejames endorses the fork is unconfirmed.

The biggest change landed in the past two weeks. **VRChat entered an open beta of native face tracking on 23 October 2026** ([UploadVR](https://www.uploadvr.com/vrchat-gets-native-face-tracking-support-in-open-beta/)), and the feature went live in client **2026.4.1 on 28 October** ([VRChat on X](https://x.com/VRChat/status/2115512628550205783)). It is built on VRCFT's **Unified Expressions** standard. External apps feed it over OSC at `/tracking/face/v1`. Synced face data no longer counts against the avatar's parameter budget, and a webcam mode called Selfie Expression covers desktop and Android ([VRChat SDK 3.10.6](https://creators.vrchat.com/releases/release-3-10-6/); [VRChat Creators: Face Tracking](https://creators.vrchat.com/avatars/face-tracking/)). The patch notes say the client syncs all 102 Unified Expressions values ([VRChat 2026.4.1](https://docs.vrchat.com/docs/vrchat-202641)). These details come from summaries of VRChat's pages, which the research could not open in full. What this means for Astra: VRCFT is no longer the only route into VRChat. It becomes one OSC source among several, so rigs and face-transfer tools should target Unified Expressions names plus ARKit aliases. **PawlygonStudio's** open Unity tool already does this matching ([PawlygonStudio README](https://github.com/PawlygonStudio/VRC-Facetracking/blob/main/README.md)). Tools that help existing avatars move to the native system are a fresh, open opportunity.

The do-it-yourself hardware scene is gathering around **Project Babble**. Its Baballonia app does "cross-platform, hardware-agnostic XR eye and face tracking" for VRChat, Resonite and ChilloutVR ([GitHub](https://github.com/Project-Babble/Baballonia); [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4091970/Project_Babble_Baballonia/)). On 26 September 2026 Babble announced a two-camera mouth tracker for Valve's Steam Frame ([hashout.jp](https://hashout.jp/gadget/11472/)). **EyeTrackVR** (1.2k stars) now runs standalone on the Steam Frame and has moved to the Babble Software Distribution License. Its ESP32 firmware, OpenIris, is by **lorow** ([GitHub](https://github.com/EyeTrackVR/EyeTrackVR)). The same handles turn up across these projects, dfgHiatus among them ([Steambase](https://steambase.io/games/project-babble-baballonia/info)). In practice this makes it one small, reachable community. Hardware is part of Astra's own story here. **Meta discontinued the Quest Pro in September 2024, stopped selling it in January 2025, and sells no face- or eye-tracking headset today** ([Road to VR](https://roadtovr.com/meta-discontinues-quest-pro/)). In late 2026 the main headset with built-in face tracking is **Samsung Galaxy XR**. It reports blendshape weights through Google's `XR_ANDROID_face_tracking` extension ([Android XR](https://developer.android.com/develop/xr/openxr/extensions/XR_ANDROID_face_tracking?hl=de)) and reaches PC VRChat through Virtual Desktop plus VRCFT ([Spacebar](https://www.spacebar.news/samsung-galaxy-xr-pc-vr-vrchat-guide/)). The alternative is an eye-tracking headset plus an add-on mouth tracker, such as HTC's **$119 Facial Tracker (38 blendshapes at 60 Hz)** ([VIVE shop](https://shop-us.vive.com/products/21045006)). Converting between Quest Pro and Galaxy XR expression sets is just the job her face-transfer tooling could do.

The VTuber world runs on the same buses (OSC and the VMC protocol), and its tools are made by long-running solo developers. **Emiliana (emilianavt)** builds VSeeFace and the open OpenSeeFace webcam tracker, and maintains a VTuber software list last updated in October 2026 ([gist](https://gist.github.com/emilianavt/cbf4d6de6f7fb01a42d4cce922795794)). The others include DenchiSoft (VTube Studio), Suvidriel (VNyan) and malaybaku (VMagicMirror) ([VNyan](https://suvidriel.itch.io/vnyan); [VMagicMirror](https://malaybaku.github.io/VMagicMirror/en/index)). Research supports the premise behind all of this work. Stanford VHIL's study of more than 200 people found that **facial expressions contributed more than body movement to conversational outcomes** in avatar conversations ([Kruzic, Kruzic, Herrera & Bailenson 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-76672-4)). A January 2026 preprint links avatar expressions to inter-brain synchrony, though it has not been peer reviewed ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.01.28.702209v1.full)). The effect is not universal. One IEEE TVCG study found that adding an avatar head did not raise social presence but the amount of facial expression did ([TVCG 2024](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1109/TVCG.2024.3372051)), and a JCMC study found no effect ([JCMC](https://academic.oup.com/jcmc/article/31/5/zmag021/8832613)). Expression intensity and avatar style appear to matter, which suits someone who builds stylised avatars.

## Labs now pipe headset blendshapes into photoreal heads and robot faces

Big-company avatar research has moved to **Gaussian-splat** heads. Meta's Codec Avatars Lab in Pittsburgh, where Shunsuke Saito keeps a publication list ([shunsukesaito.github.io](https://shunsukesaito.github.io/)), showed **URAvatar** at SIGGRAPH Asia 2024. URAvatar is a universal model trained on hundreds of light-stage scans, which is then tuned into a personal, relightable avatar from **a phone scan** ([project page](https://junxuan-li.github.io/urgca-website/)). Apple confirmed in October 2025 that Vision Pro Personas use Gaussian splatting ([Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/apple-confirms-personas-use-gaussian-splatting)). The academic paper closest to Astra's work is **OFERA** (IEEE VR 2026) from **Woontack Woo's KAIST lab**. It drives photoreal Gaussian heads in real time from **commercial headset blendshape signals**, the same kind of data she gets from VRCFT ([arXiv 2602.01748](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01748)). NVIDIA open-sourced **Audio2Face and Audio2Emotion** in September 2025, including the framework for training them on custom characters ([GitHub](https://github.com/NVIDIA/Audio2Face-3D-SDK)). That gives a speech-driven backup for mouth shapes on headsets that track only the eyes.

Physical robot faces have followed a clear path at **Columbia's Creative Machines Lab (Hod Lipson, Yuhang Hu)**. **EVA** (2021) mirrored a person's expressions using about **$900 in parts** ([Creative Machines Lab](https://www.creativemachineslab.com/eva.html)). **Emo** (Science Robotics, 2024) uses 26 actuators under silicone skin, predicts a coming smile **about 839 ms before it happens**, and smiles at the same moment as the person, because delayed mimicry reads as insincere ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2024/03/240327154854.htm); [Columbia](https://www.engineering.columbia.edu/about/news/robot-can-you-say-cheese)). In January 2026 a Science Robotics paper described a face robot that first learned what its 26 motors do **by watching itself in a mirror**, then learned human lip motion from YouTube video ([Columbia](https://www.engineering.columbia.edu/about/news/robot-learns-lip-sync)). Several secondary sources say Hu founded the Shanghai face-robot startup AheadForm ([Mike Kalil](https://mikekalil.com/blog/aheadform-elf-robot/)), whose Origin M1 head uses about 25 micro motors ([Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/technology/robotics/chinese-company-develops-creepy-ultra-lifelike-robot-face-watch-it-blink-twitch-and-nod)). Columbia's mirror step is the same calibration problem Astra would face in mapping VRCFT parameters onto servos.

Several teams already run her exact data path into hardware. **Honda Research Institute Europe** (Chao Wang, Michael Gienger, Fan Zhang) uses a **Unity app on a Meta Quest Pro** to teleoperate a robot with LED eyes, moving ears, a neck and arms. Of the Quest Pro's roughly 70 blendshapes, the team uses only **the 7 that most affect perceived emotion**, and a learned model trained on these demonstrations then generates expressions on its own in real time ([arXiv 2508.08999](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08999v2)). Japan's **Yui** android uses the HTC Facial Tracker to drive 28 facial actuation points ([arXiv 2312.06310](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.06310v1)). A June 2026 paper describes a hand-tuned mapping from headset blendshape weights for its field deployment ([arXiv 2606.08099](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.08099)). The Italian Institute of Technology's **iCub3** (Daniele Pucci) retargets an operator's gaze, eyelids and mouth from about 400 km away ([arXiv 2203.06972](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.06972v2)). Sven Behnke's **NimbRo** team (Bonn) won the **$5M ANA Avatar XPRIZE** in 2022, partly by animating a photo of the operator's face from cameras inside the headset ([XPRIZE](https://go.xprize.org/news/ana-avatar-xprize-winners); [IROS 2022](https://www.ais.uni-bonn.de/papers/IROS_2022_Rochow.pdf)). **ExFace** turns human blendshapes into motor commands at 60 fps within 0.15 s ([arXiv 2504.14477](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14477v1)). Tsinghua's **Morpheus** face publishes its hardware and code ([arXiv 2507.16645](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16645v1)), and **Rena** learns face-to-servo mapping by imitation, with 4.4% servo error ([arXiv 2510.23059](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23059v1)).

For a solo builder, a face-tracked head is realistic at **about $300 to $1,000 in parts**. A 2025 Fab Academy student project in Puebla came to about $300 for a 16-servo head with a Raspberry Pi 5 ([Fab Academy, Osorio](https://fabacademy.org/2025/labs/puebla/students/javier-osorio/finalproject.html); [Fab Academy, Vivar](https://fabacademy.org/2025/labs/puebla/students/erwin-vivar/final-project.html)). Maker Will Cogley's open animatronic eye designs are widely reused ([brainforest/robot-head](https://github.com/brainforest/robot-head); [Arduino Blog](https://blog.arduino.cc/2024/12/09/this-frighteningly-realistic-animatronic-head-features-expressive-silicone-skin/)). The InMoov project warns that cheap SG90 servos wear out quickly ([InMoov i2Head](https://inmoov.fr/i2head-for-inmoov/)). VRCFT's own documentation says what its parameters control "is completely up to you" ([GitHub](https://github.com/benaclejames/VRCFaceTracking)). Even so, no one has published a VRCFT-to-servo bridge. The build would be an OSC listener in Python feeding an Arduino or ESP32 with a PCA9685 driver and 8 to 12 servos for the eyes, lids, brows, jaw and mouth corners, calibrated with a "mirror" step and smoothed with a low-pass filter. A stylised or LED face sidesteps the uncanny valley. Viewers praised AheadForm's ultra-realistic head and also called it creepy, mostly for its blinking and fixed gaze ([Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/technology/robotics/chinese-company-develops-creepy-ultra-lifelike-robot-face-watch-it-blink-twitch-and-nod)). Commercial realistic heads cost **$150,000 to $175,000** (Realbotix Aria) ([Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/photo-story/meet-aria-humanoid-robot-girlfriend)).

## AI characters mostly hear voices, which leaves the face-reading pet unclaimed

Most "emotional" AI character platforms read the user's **voice, not the face**. Hume's EVI, Inworld's voice runtime and NVIDIA's Audio2Emotion all work from speech, and this research found no evidence that EVI or Inworld take facial input ([Hume products](https://www.hume.ai/products); [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/08/13/3132320/0/en/inworld-runtime-the-first-ai-runtime-for-consumer-applications.html); [NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/nvidia-audio2face-is-now-open-source/345978)). Convai's "vision" feature sees the game scene from the character's point of view, not the player's webcam ([Convai docs](https://docs.convai.com/api-docs/plugins-and-integrations/convai-unreal-engine-plugin/features/vision)). Hume shut down its original face-and-voice Expression Measurement API on 14 June 2026 ([Hume FAQ](https://dev.hume.ai/docs/expression-measurement/faq)) and opened a limited-release successor for live video on 6 October 2026 ([Hume changelog](https://dev.hume.ai/expression-measurement/changelog/2026/10/6.md)). In January 2026, Google DeepMind licensed Hume's technology and hired its CEO Alan Cowen ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/22/google-reportedly-snags-up-team-behind-ai-voice-startup-hume-ai)). The products that do watch faces are video-call avatars and hardware companions. Tavus's Raven models track the user's eye contact and expressions during calls ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/03/06/tavus-introduces-family-ai-models-power-real-time-human-face-face-interaction/)). **Razer's Project AVA**, shown at CES 2026, is a holographic desk companion with an HD camera and eye tracking ([Razer](https://www.razer.com/newsroom/product-news/project-ava/)). It is the closest commercial match to her desktop pets. Soul Machines, the best-known "digital human" firm, went into receivership in February 2026 and was bought by AppDirect in September ([NBR](https://www.nbr.co.nz/tech/soul-machines-in-receivership); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/09/28/appdirect-acquires-interactive-ai-avatar-firm-soul-machines-for-advisors-and-businesses/)).

The open-source scene is closer to her than any company. **Open-LLM-VTuber** is an offline Live2D companion with camera input, a transparent **desktop pet mode** and Claude support ([GitHub](https://github.com/Open-LLM-VTuber/Open-LLM-VTuber)). **Project AIRI** (moeru-ai) is a "soul container" with an Electron "Tamagotchi" desktop build and about 35k stars ([GitHub](https://github.com/moeru-ai/airi); [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/project-airi/about)). For VRChat specifically, **uezo's aiavatarkit** drives avatar facial expressions from an LLM over OSC ([SourcePulse](https://www.sourcepulse.org/projects/1833526)), and **Krekun's vrchat-mcp-osc** lets Claude Desktop control a VRChat avatar ([mcpservers.org](https://mcpservers.org/servers/Krekun/vrchat-mcp-osc)). Researchers have also put a GPT-4 NPC with matching facial expressions inside VRChat ([Nweon](https://paper.nweon.com/?p=15770)). All of these send expression *out* to an avatar. None of the projects found takes the user's VRCFT face data *in* and uses it to set an AI character's mood. Astra's stack already produces that missing signal, so linking her face-in pipeline to an OSC face-out bridge would create a two-way loop that no one appears to have published. Meta's open **Seamless Interaction** models are the nearest research. They generate a listener's facial reactions from the other person's face and voice, and can drive 3D avatars ([Meta](https://ai.meta.com/research/seamless-interaction/)).

Both the science and the law point the same design direction: react to facial signals, and don't announce an emotion. The key review by Barrett and colleagues (2019) concluded that a facial configuration such as a scowl often signals something other than a specific emotion ([APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/emotional-expressions-reconsidered-challenges-to-inferring-emotion-from-human-facial-movements.html)). Microsoft retired Azure's emotion inference in 2022, citing the lack of scientific consensus on defining emotions ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2022/06/21/microsoft-restricts-access-controversial-ai-facial-recognition-technology/)). Since **2 February 2025, the EU AI Act bans emotion recognition from biometric data in workplaces and schools** ([Future of Privacy Forum](https://fpf.org/blog/red-lines-under-eu-ai-act-unpacking-the-prohibition-of-emotion-recognition-in-the-workplace-and-education-institutions/)). The Commission's guidelines say that detecting a smile does not count as emotion recognition ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/02/17/understanding-the-eu-ai-acts-prohibited-practices-key-workplace-and-advertising-102k011)). A home hobby project falls outside the ban, though a distributed pet that labels users' emotions could carry transparency duties (this is an inference, not legal advice). Shipped games already work this way. *Before Your Eyes* advances its story on real blinks ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Before_Your_Eyes)), and *Nevermind* adapts its horror to heart rate and webcam signs of distress ([Affectiva](https://www.affectiva.com/success-story/flying-mollusk/)). So a pet should respond to raw weights such as a smile, a lowered brow, blinks and gaze. Privacy matters too. Vivek Nair's Berkeley team **identified more than 50,000 VR users from head and hand motion alone** ([Berkeley EECS](https://eecs.berkeley.edu/news/eecs-researchers-explore-unprecedented-privacy-risks-vr/)), so Astra's logged face streams should be treated as biometric data. Sending only numbers, never images, to an LLM, as the hobby project EmoChat does with labels, is the safe pattern ([EmoChat](https://www.mintlify.com/Dani-zm/emotion-recognition-ai/quickstart)).

## Euler's formula is the grammar of every quantum gate, not the engine

The terms need sorting out first. **Euler's formula** is e^(iθ) = cos θ + i sin θ, for any angle θ. **Euler's identity**, e^(iπ) + 1 = 0, is one value of it: a half-turn, which multiplies by −1. Quantum computing uses the formula everywhere and the identity at specific, important moments. A quantum state lists one complex number, an *amplitude*, for each possible outcome. Euler's formula splits each amplitude into a length and an angle. The length squared is the probability, and the angle, the *phase* e^(iφ), cannot be seen in any single measurement. It decides how alternatives interfere. Picture each amplitude as an arrow. When two paths lead to the same outcome, their arrows add: arrows pointing the same way reinforce, and arrows a half-turn apart (differing by e^(iπ) = −1) cancel. The standard single-qubit form, |ψ⟩ = cos(θ/2)|0⟩ + e^(iφ) sin(θ/2)|1⟩, comes straight from Euler's formula, and the Bloch sphere picture is built on it ([arXiv 2503.19109](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.19109)).

Every quantum gate is an Euler rotation. All closed-system quantum dynamics is a matrix exponential, U = e^(−iHt/ħ), and "Hamiltonian simulation" is defined as building that U ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_simulation)). For the Pauli matrices, which square to the identity, the exponential is **literally Euler's formula with a matrix in place of the number i**: e^(iθA) = cos θ·I + i sin θ·A ([problem set](https://www.ilorentz.org/QI/problemset_linalg_QI.pdf)). IBM's Qiskit writes its rotation gates in exactly this e^(−iθP/2) form ([Qiskit RZZ docs](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/api/qiskit/0.31/qiskit.circuit.library.RZZGate)). The **Z gate is diag(1, e^(iπ))**, Euler's identity in the form of a gate, and turning a qubit a full 360° about Z gives −I, a factor of −1 again. 3Blue1Brown explains why e^(it) moves in a circle: a point whose velocity is always i times its position goes halfway round to −1 after π seconds ([3Blue1Brown](https://www.3blue1brown.com/lessons/eulers-formula-dynamically)). Schrödinger's equation for a state of definite energy is the same equation. So every energy component of a quantum system runs its own "Euler clock" at speed E/ħ, and everything measurable comes from the angles *between* the clock hands.

The identity itself sits at the heart of interference. The **Hadamard gate is the 2-point quantum Fourier transform**, H = (1/√2)[[1, 1], [1, e^(iπ)]]. The N-th roots of unity always sum to zero, and for N = 2 that sum *is* 1 + e^(iπ) = 0. So Euler's identity is exactly the cancellation that lets a Hadamard turn phase into a measurable bit. Larger QFTs use the entries e^(2πijk/N), and quantum phase estimation reads out the angle of an eigenvalue e^(2πiθ). These are the building blocks of Shor's algorithm and of physics simulation ([IBM Quantum Learning](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/learning/en/courses/fundamentals-of-quantum-algorithms/phase-estimation-and-factoring/phase-estimation-procedure)). The Hadamard test gives outcome 0 with probability cos²(φ/2), so when the phase is e^(iπ) the ancilla qubit reports Euler's −1 with certainty ([Qulacs](https://dojo.qulacs.org/en/latest/notebooks/2.2_Hadamard_test.html); [PennyLane](https://pennylane.ai/demos/tutorial_phase_kickback)). Simulation algorithms chain many small Euler rotations. Seth Lloyd proved in 1996 that this "Trotter" product approach efficiently simulates any *local* quantum system ([arXiv 1004.5528](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1004.5528)). A numerical check run for this research shows how the method behaves: for H = X + Z, the error falls from 0.80 with one step to 0.011 with 64 steps, roughly as 1/n. The smallest demo that makes the identity visible fits in a few lines. In the circuit H → P(θ) → H on |0⟩, the amplitude of outcome 0 is (1 + e^(iθ))/2, so at θ = π it equals (e^(iπ) + 1)/2 = 0, and outcome 1 becomes certain:

```python
# numpy, tested 2026-11-03: the "Euler slider"
import numpy as np
H = np.array([[1, 1], [1, -1]]) / np.sqrt(2)
for theta in np.linspace(0, 2*np.pi, 9):
    P = np.diag([1, np.exp(1j*theta)])            # phase gate = Euler's formula on |1>
    out = H @ P @ H @ np.array([1, 0])
    print(f"{theta/np.pi:.2f}π  amp0={(1+np.exp(1j*theta))/2:.3f}  P(1)={abs(out[1])**2:.3f}")
```

Calling Euler's identity "the foundation" goes too far in four ways. First, it is one value of a general formula and contains no physics. It doesn't say which Hamiltonian describes the universe, and the Hamiltonian is where all the physics lives. Second, it is not where the speedup comes from. Scott Aaronson notes that **real-number amplitudes typically suffice for quantum computing** ([Shtetl-Optimized](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=4021)). The power comes from an n-qubit state having 2^n amplitudes, the exponential bookkeeping that Feynman's 1982 paper pointed to ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02650179)). Third, whether nature *needs* complex numbers is an open debate. Experiments in 2022 ruled out real-number quantum theory under the standard rule for combining systems. One superconducting-qubit test scored 8.09 against a real-number limit of 7.66 ([Chen et al., PRL 2022](https://physics.aps.org/featured-article-pdf/10.1103/PhysRevLett.128.040403); [Wu et al., PRL 2022](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevLett.129.140401); [Renou et al. 2021](https://web3.arxiv.org/abs/2101.10873)). But a June 2026 PRL paper and a 2025 preprint built real-number theories with a different combining rule that predict the same results ([Barrios Hita et al.](https://nachrichten.idw-online.de/2026/06/19/physics-publication-in-physical-review-letters-does-quantum-mechanics-need-complex-numbers); [Hoffreumon & Woods](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02808v2)). The two results do not conflict. Complex numbers remain the natural, universal working language, and every SDK uses them. Fourth, the foundation also needs superposition and the tensor product, the Born rule, measurement and decoherence, the Hamiltonian, and error correction. A framing that stays accurate and keeps Euler at the centre: *"Every quantum gate is an Euler rotation, and e^(iπ) = −1 is the half-turn that makes alternatives cancel."* This research found **no project, paper or artwork that explicitly makes Euler's identity the organising core of quantum computing**. Bloch-sphere visualisers exist but never mention it ([FastHTML gallery](https://gallery.fastht.ml/vizualizations/bloch_sphere/display); [BlochSphereVisualizer](https://github.com/hmasum52/BlochSphereVisualizer)). The niche is open.

## Nobody simulates the universe; about 100 qubits run toy universes

Feynman's founding argument (1982) was that describing a quantum system on an ordinary computer takes memory that grows exponentially with the number of particles, so the simulator should itself be quantum ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02650179)). In game-engine terms: in a classical engine, adding a particle adds a few floats to the save file, while in an exact quantum simulation, adding one qubit-sized particle *doubles* it. Physicists do not apply this idea to "the universe". They apply it to specific model Hamiltonians, especially the lattice gauge theories behind electromagnetism and the strong force. In those theories, real-time dynamics and dense matter run into the "sign problem", where classical sampling blows up exponentially. Two roadmaps set out the field: Bauer, Davoudi and about 30 co-authors ([PRX Quantum 2023](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PRXQuantum.4.027001)), and the CERN/DESY/IBM working group, which says the qubit counts needed are "largely beyond" today's noisy devices ([Di Meglio et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.03236)).

The table shows how big the toy universes are as of late 2026. Each step forward adds a dimension (1+1D to 2+1D), moves from simpler "Abelian" theories to the "non-Abelian" kind behind the strong force, or goes from static states to collisions. Work from 2025 and 2026 took all three steps in small form, and a September 2026 thesis reports what it calls the first numerical evidence of inelastic particle production in simulated collisions ([arXiv 2609.28825](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28825)). None of these simulations has been shown to beat classical computers at particle physics. The US Department of Energy says only that future simulations are *expected* to surpass classical computing ([DOE](https://www.energy.gov/science/np/articles/nuclear-physicists-create-scalable-quantum-circuits-simulate-fundamental)), even though some headlines claimed more ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2025/11/251118220104.htm)). The cosmology "universes" are analogs: sound waves in ultracold gas follow the same equations as quantum fields in expanding spacetime. Heidelberg's lab universe expanded **by a factor of less than 3, against the 10^26 that inflation theory assumes** ([Physics Today](https://pubs.aip.org/physicstoday/online/41858/The-early-universe-in-a-quantum-gas)), and gravity is never dynamical. The 2022 "wormhole" is the cautionary tale. Independent physicists argued that its machine-learned 9-qubit model showed artifacts of its own small size, not gravity ([arXiv 2308.00697](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.00697); [Woit](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~woit/wordpress/?p=13425)). The lesson for Astra is to check what is actually being simulated, not the headline.

Some limits do not depend on engineering. Seth Lloyd calculated that the observable universe could have performed at most **about 10^120 operations on about 10^90 bits** (about 10^120 bits if gravity is included). Lloyd framed these numbers as the *minimum* resources needed to simulate the universe on a quantum computer ([arXiv quant-ph/0110141](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0110141)). A computer built inside the universe has fewer degrees of freedom than the universe itself, so a full-resolution self-simulation is ruled out by counting before engineering even starts. Franco Vazza added energy costs in 2025. The paper concluded that even a low-resolution simulation of *Earth* would need energy that is "either incompatible with physics or astronomically large" ([Frontiers in Physics](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physics/articles/10.3389/fphy.2025.1561873/pdf)), though critics say "impossible" overstates it ([Lincoln Cannon](https://lincoln.metacannon.net/2025/05/vazza-overstates-constraints-on-simulation.html)). David Wolpert's computer-science result is purely logical. Nothing in logic forbids a universe from simulating itself; the binding limits are physical ([Mapping Ignorance](https://mappingignorance.org/2026/01/06/simulation-hypothesis/)). Two further facts stand even with a perfect machine: quantum mechanics forbids copying an unknown state, so the universe's present state could never be loaded in, and chaos would amplify any error. The people actually doing this work include Martin Savage's InQubator for Quantum Simulation, which posts its papers ([IQuS](https://iqus.uw.edu/publications/)); the CERN Quantum Technology Initiative ([arXiv 2511.03883](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.03883)); Fermilab's SQMS centre, renewed in November 2025 with **$125M over five years** ([Fermilab](https://news.fnal.gov/2025/11/fermilabs-sqms-center-funded-with-125-million-to-shape-the-future-of-quantum-information-science/)); and the Simons "It from Qubit" collaboration on spacetime and entanglement ([Simons Foundation](https://www.simonsfoundation.org/collaborations/it-from-qubit/)).

## Full Standard Model physics sits decades past IBM's 2029 machine

Error correction now works in the lab, but useful fault-tolerant machines are still promises. Google's Willow chip (December 2024) halved its logical error rate each time its code grew ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/research/google-willow-quantum-chip/)). Quantinuum's 98-qubit **Helios** launched with **48 error-corrected logical qubits** ([Quantinuum](https://www.quantinuum.com/products-solutions/quantinuum-systems/helios)) and reached a record logical error rate of about 5×10⁻⁶ per round in October 2026 ([HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/2026/10/19/quantinuum-says-helios-results-strengthen-its-path-to-apollo/)). Harvard and QuEra encoded **up to 96 logical qubits** in neutral atoms ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/neutral-atom-quantum-computing)). IBM promises **Starling in 2029 (200 logical qubits, 100 million gates)** and **Blue Jay in 2033 or later (2,000 logical qubits, 1 billion operations)** ([IBM](https://www.ibm.com/quantum/blog/large-scale-ftqc)). Dates slip, though. As of 22 October 2026 there was no public demonstration of IBM's 2026 Kookaburra chip ([Quantum Investor Notebook](https://www.quantuminvestornotebook.com/scorecards/ibm)), and IBM usually updates its roadmap in November, so this could change within weeks. Rigetti's 108-qubit system arrived months late ([The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/01/13/rigetti-delays-108-qubit-cepheus-system/)). Nature's editors said Microsoft's Majorana paper "does not represent evidence for Majorana zero modes" ([Science](https://www.science.org/content/article/debate-erupts-around-microsoft-s-blockbuster-quantum-computing-claims)). The best independent check is DARPA's Quantum Benchmarking Initiative, which asks whether any approach reaches utility scale **by 2033**. In October 2026 it moved Atom Computing, Diraq, IBM and IonQ into its final stage, joining Microsoft and PsiQuantum ([Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/darpa-selects-atom-computing-diraq-ibm-and-ionq-for-final-stage-c-verification-under-quantum-benchmarking-initiative/)).

| Milestone | Logical qubits | Operations | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best noisy runs today (IBM Heron, Schwinger model) | none (112 physical) | Trotterised time evolution | Done, 2024–2026 |
| Quantinuum Helios; Harvard/QuEra arrays | 48; up to 96 | Record low error; below-threshold suppression | Delivered 2025–2026 |
| Microsoft / Atom Computing "Magne" ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/neutral-atom-quantum-computing)) | 50 | — | Promised for start of 2027 |
| IBM Starling; Quantinuum Apollo ([Quantinuum](https://www.quantinuum.com/press-releases/quantinuum-reports-second-quarter-2026-results)) | 200; "fully fault-tolerant" | 10^8 (Starling) | Promised 2029 |
| IBM Blue Jay | 2,000 | 10^9 | Promised 2033+ |
| 64-site 1+1D Schwinger model, fault-tolerant ([Dalzell et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.03011)) | ~333 | 5×10^13 T gates per shot | Beyond Blue Jay's gate budget |
| 3+1D U(1), SU(2), SU(3) gauge theories ([Dalzell et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.03011)) | 10^4–10^8 | 10^17–10^56 T gates | No published date |
| The whole observable universe ([Lloyd](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0110141)) | ≥10^90 bits | ≥10^120 ops | Physically ruled out |

Estimates for the real target, 3+1D quantum chromodynamics (QCD, the theory of quarks and gluons), are falling fast but remain enormous. An improved method put the total at **1.7×10^27 T gates** ([arXiv 2503.11888](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.11888)). An August 2026 paper needs about **12·L³ logical qubits** for an L×L×L lattice ([arXiv 2608.17115](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.17115)). For the L = 32 lattices that QCD practitioners usually want, that is about 400,000 logical qubits. Assume an optimistic 10^6 to 10^9 logical T gates per second, and 10^27 gates take 10^18 to 10^21 seconds, longer than the universe's age of about 4×10^17 seconds. So even today's best estimate needs many more orders of magnitude of algorithmic improvement. Industry overviews expect early fault-tolerant demonstrations in **2028–2029** and commercially valuable machines in the **early to mid 2030s**, with "substantial uncertainty" ([The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/09/04/main-challenges-facing-quantum-computing/)). Aaronson, long a sceptic of hype, wrote in August 2026 that "the reality of quantum advantage is no longer a live question" but that usefulness still is ([Shtetl-Optimized](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=9979)). Gil Kalai still doubts that quantum computers can scale at all ([Kalai](https://gilkalai.wordpress.com/2026/03/10/scott-aaronsons-view-of-my-view-about-quantum-computing/)). My assessment from these sources, since no roadmap gives dates: beyond-classical results for 2+1D gauge theories and large 1+1D collision simulations could come between the late 2020s and early 2030s; small non-Abelian ("strong force"-like) dynamics in the 2030s; real QCD observables in the 2040s or later; and a full universe never. Waiting for hardware alone is a plan measured in decades, and no amount of personal money shortens it.

## Starting costs $0, and skill is the bottleneck, not money

Nobody will sell an individual a useful quantum computer, and nobody needs to. The only machines for sale are SpinQ's 2-qubit teaching devices, at about **$8,700** for the Gemini Mini ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/you-can-buy-a-portable-quantum-computer-for-under-dollar9k/)) and about **$44,000** for the Gemini Lab. One 2026 guide says these "cannot run anything you could not simulate on a phone" ([quantumcomputingcost.com](https://quantumcomputingcost.com/quantum-computer-price-list/)). Every useful machine is reached through the cloud, so "when I can afford it" really means "when I rent time on a better cloud backend". No fault-tolerant machine has published cloud pricing yet. A state vector needs 2^n amplitudes, so 31 qubits in single precision takes about 16 GiB. That fits on a 24 GB card, matching community benchmarks ([blueqat](https://blueqat.com/yuichiro_minato2/74608c73-a292-462a-b10e-ad594283d04e)). Each added qubit doubles the memory. For the one- to ten-qubit Euler demos and small toy universes she would build first, her own PC beats a noisy cloud machine.

The free learning path that builds most directly on Euler's formula starts with 3Blue1Brown's e^(iπ) dynamics lesson ([3Blue1Brown](https://www.3blue1brown.com/lessons/eulers-formula-dynamically)), then moves to Quantum Country, Andy Matuschak and Michael Nielsen's spaced-repetition primer ([Quantum Zeitgeist](https://quantumzeitgeist.com/top-10-free-resources-for-quantum-computing/)), and then to running the Euler-slider and Hadamard-test demos above. From there, IBM Quantum Learning's Basics of Quantum Information leads into its QFT and phase-estimation lessons ([IBM QPE lesson](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/learning/en/courses/utility-scale-quantum-computing/quantum-phase-estimation)), and 3Blue1Brown's 2025 Grover video, which Aaronson praised for dispelling the "tries all possibilities in parallel" myth, shows signs and amplitudes at work in a real algorithm ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RQWpF2Gb-gU)). The first toy universe is then within reach: Trotterised time evolution of an Ising chain, followed by the Schwinger model, on her own GPU, checked against the IQuS papers that ran the same model on 112 real qubits. The PennyLane Codebook and Microsoft's Quantum Katas supply drills along the way, Nielsen & Chuang is the standard reference ([book list](https://github.com/poig/Qc_book_list)), and John Watrous's 2025 text is a free alternative ([arXiv 2507.11536](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.11536)).

Her Unity and VR skills are her way into the quantum community, because the projects that fill the open Euler niche all run on classical simulators: a VR "Euler slider" whose dial makes the arrows 1 and e^(iθ) visibly cancel at π, "Euler clocks" for a two-level system, a Bloch-sphere view of Trotter steps converging on the exact path, or a desktop pet that flips its sign through a real H–Z–H circuit. The community is built for people without an institution. The **Unitary Foundation gives $4,000 microgrants** for open-source and educational quantum projects ([Unitary Foundation](https://unitary.foundation/grants)). QOSF runs a free mentorship program for "quantum enthusiasts" ([QOSF](https://qosf.org/qc_mentorship/)). IBM's Qiskit Advocate program needs its roughly $200 developer certification to move past the entry tier ([IBM blog](https://ibm.com/quantum/blog/qiskit-advocate-program); [certification](https://www.ibm.com/training/certification/ibm-certified-quantum-computation-using-qiskit-v2x-developer-associate-C9008400)). **MIT iQuHACK 2026 drew more than 1,400 participants from 76 countries**, most of them online ([The Tech](https://thetech.com/2026/02/19/iquhack-2026)), and QHack 2026 had a $67,000 prize pool ([QHack](https://qhack.ai/)). Qiskit itself says people can contribute "with just a few basic web development skills" ([Qiskit on X](https://x.com/qiskit/status/1521853593686614017)). A VR visualiser of Euler-rotation toy universes is a strong microgrant pitch, and it is the one project where her two interests become one portfolio.

## Conclusion

The gaps this research found in both halves of Astra's question share a shape. The hardware, the core algorithms and the big labs already exist. What is missing is translation and visualisation. No published tool takes VRCFT data into a pet's mood or into servos. No quantum tool or explainer found is organised around Euler's identity. The niches that are open to a self-taught builder are the connections between communities, not the frontier inside one of them. Both of her ambitions also get *more* reachable when stated honestly. A pet that responds to a raw smile weight is sounder science, and safer under EU law, than one that claims to read sadness. Simulating toy universes defined by real Hamiltonians is what working physicists actually do, while "our universe" is ruled out by counting.

That shift changes what "someday" means. The date Astra cannot control, when fault-tolerant hardware arrives, matters less than the date she can: when she has a toy-universe model, in code, ready to send to whatever cloud backend exists. The people who will use 2029-era machines for physics are the ones writing Schwinger-model circuits on today's 100-qubit hardware. With $0 hardware access and a GPU that holds 31 qubits, she can join them this month, and the Euler-centred VR tools she would build along the way give the quantum community something it currently lacks.
