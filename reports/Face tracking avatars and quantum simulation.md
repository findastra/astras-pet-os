## Cloning yourself is behaviour cloning, and no one found has cloned their own reactions from VR face logs

What Astra described, "basically clone myself in VR chat", is a known kind of machine learning with a plain name. **Behaviour cloning** is "the most basic form of imitation learning": record an expert's situations and the actions they took, then learn to map one to the other, with no trial and error ([arXiv 2512.08052](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08052); [UPenn CIS 5190](https://www.seas.upenn.edu/~cis5190/spring2023/docs/lecture19.pdf); search summary). Here the expert is Astra, the situation is what is happening around her in VRChat, and the action is what her face does next.

Face researchers call the same task **listener reaction generation** or **facial reaction generation**. The REACT challenge series formalises it as Multiple Appropriate Facial Reaction Generation: given what a speaker does, generate several reactions that would all fit, not one "right" answer, and score them on how well they match real reactions and how realistic they look ([REACT 2025, arXiv 2505.17223](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17223v1); search summary). **REACT 2026 added official personalised tracks**, offline and online, for reactions that match one specific person ([arXiv 2606.07935](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.07935); search summary). Astra's plan is the personalised online track, done as behaviour cloning with herself as the only expert.

### A handful of labs clone one person's reactions, all from video

| Project | Who | What it shows for Astra | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Learning to Listen (CVPR 2022) | Evonne Ng and co-authors (UC Berkeley, Meta) | One model per listener, built from 6 YouTube channels; person-specific models released for four listeners; training the motion codebook took "~2 days on 4 GPUs" | [learning2listen README](https://github.com/evonneng/learning2listen) |
| Can Language Models Learn to Listen? (ICCV 2023) | Ng et al. | Face motion treated as tokens for a language model, "from the idea that each person has a characteristic way of listening"; for a listener with less data, a codebook shared across people beat a personal one | [arXiv 2308.10897](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.10897) (search summary) |
| Audio2Photoreal (CVPR 2024) | Ng et al., Meta | Person-specific face, body and hand motion for 4 captured people | [arXiv 2401.01885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01885) (search summary) |
| PerFRDiff (2024) and PerReactor (AAAI-25) | REACT community | A generic many-person model personalised from the target's past facial behaviour | [Exeter repository](https://ore.exeter.ac.uk/repository/handle/10871/137834); [AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/32159/34314) (search summary) |
| Seamless Interaction (2025) | Meta FAIR | 4,000+ hours of face-to-face interaction from 4,000+ people (CC-BY-NC); its model reacts to the partner's face and voice | [GitHub](https://github.com/facebookresearch/seamless_interaction); [arXiv 2506.22554](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.22554v2) |
| Generative agents of 1,052 people | Joon Sung Park and colleagues (Stanford) | A two-hour interview per person; the agents matched people's survey answers 85% as well as the people matched their own answers two weeks later (attitudes, not faces) | [arXiv 2411.10109](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109v1) (search summary) |

Two lessons come out of this table. First, **every working personalised method starts from a generic model trained on many people and then adapts it** to the target, and with little data, sharing beats going solo. Astra has only her own data, so her clone should be "sensible default behaviour plus Astra's own reactions", not Astra alone. Second, all of these learn from faces extracted from video. The nearest precedent that uses headset face data drives a robot, not an avatar. **Honda Research Institute Europe** (Chao Wang, Michael Gienger, Fan Zhang) recorded Quest Pro face blendshapes, with head and controller poses, as demonstrations labelled with emotions such as fear, curiosity and happiness. A flow-matching model trained on them then generates the robot's expressions on its own, in response to cues such as a moving object ([arXiv 2508.08999](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08999v2); search summary; see the robots section). That is learning from VR face logs, but of labelled emotions demonstrated for a robot, not one person's natural social reactions. **This research found no one who has cloned one person's own natural social reactions from their VR face-tracking logs into an avatar**, which is exactly what the data Astra already streams could do.

Plain behaviour cloning has one known failure. At run time the clone ends up in situations its own actions caused, which the recordings rarely cover, so small mistakes pile up. The standard fix, DAgger (Ross, Gordon and Bagnell, 2011), lets the learner act and has the expert label what it *should* have done ([arXiv 2512.08052](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08052); search summary). For Astra that becomes a loop she can actually run: watch a replay of AI Astra, mark "I would have done this instead", and add the mark as new data.

### Retrieval fits AI Astra's framework-free mind

AI Astra's mind has no PyTorch, TensorFlow or JAX in its core, and every reference implementation of the neural reaction models depends on PyTorch ([baseline_react2025](https://github.com/reactmultimodalchallenge/baseline_react2025); [learning2listen](https://github.com/evonneng/learning2listen)). The method that fits is older and simpler: **motion matching**. Simon Clavet's GDC 2016 talk on Ubisoft's *For Honor* described searching the whole motion-capture database every frame for the clip that best fits the moment, deliberately by brute force ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022985/Motion-Matching-and-The-Road); [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/programming/most-inspiring-game-animation-tech-talks-of-2016); search summary). Ubisoft later replaced it with learned parts because it "scales poorly in terms of data" ([Ubisoft La Forge](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/studio/laforge/news/3NWqwcMU9lMfumgfPAEfka/learned-motion-matching); search summary). That is a memory problem one person's few hours of face data do not have.

The face version is "find my recorded reaction whose moment best matches now, and play it". Reaction papers already use exactly this as a baseline, called "NN motion" ([ReactFace, arXiv 2305.15748](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.15748); search summary), and neural models report beating it ([ReactMotion](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2603.15083); search summary). Each deep-learning part has a small classical stand-in:

| What deep models use | Framework-free stand-in | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| A VQ-VAE codebook of face motion ([learning2listen](https://github.com/evonneng/learning2listen)) | k-means clusters of face snapshots (Face Codec face frames) | Coarser faces, and visible jumps between snapshots unless blended |
| A language model over motion tokens ([arXiv 2308.10897](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.10897)) | n-grams over face-frame tokens | Loses long-range timing |
| Learned onset, peak and fade timing | Hidden Markov models, which reached 89.2% event recognition on 27 facial action units ([Koelstra et al. 2010](https://www.ibug.doc.ic.ac.uk/media/uploads/documents/PAMI-2010-KoelstraEtAl-CAMERA.pdf); search summary) | Hand-built states |
| A diffusion or transformer generator | Nearest-neighbour retrieval of whole reactions (motion matching) | Can only replay reactions she has actually made |

The trade is honest. Retrieval needs little data, every choice is explainable ("she smiled because this moment resembled reaction 212"), any recording can be deleted on request, and the output can be clamped to an allowlist of expressions. It cannot invent a reaction Astra never made. Taking the top few matches and picking one with a little randomness also meets REACT's "several appropriate reactions" requirement, and that is where random bits enter (see the randomness section below).

### Record her side first, and judge the clone against Astra herself

Research datasets record the other person's audio (often with a transcript and who is speaking when), the other person's face and the reactor's face. Richer sets add emotion scores, personality and the reactor's own notes on how they felt ([seamless_interaction](https://github.com/facebookresearch/seamless_interaction); [learning2listen](https://github.com/evonneng/learning2listen); [baseline_react2025](https://github.com/reactmultimodalchallenge/baseline_react2025)). The other person's voice is the most predictive signal, and it is also the one that needs their consent. A first version can record only Astra's side plus context she controls:

| Signal | In research datasets | Astra's first version |
|---|---|---|
| Her face | Face-model or action-unit values per video frame | Her own Unified Expressions and eye values from VRCFaceTracking |
| What happened | Transcripts, speaker turns, annotated contexts | A line she types to mark a moment, plus VRChat log event types (someone joined, world entered, avatar switch, video started) |
| How she felt | First-person internal-state notes (Seamless Interaction) | Her own good or bad rating when she reviews |
| The other person | Their audio, face and transcript | Left out until they agree |

VRChat's own log is free context. It writes `output_log_*.txt` files to the AppData LocalLow VRChat folder ([VRChat Wiki](https://wiki.vrchat.com/wiki/Translations:Guides:Connection_issues/45/en)), and the community tool VRCX parses them for players joining and leaving, world changes, avatar switches, screenshots, video playback and the line `Could not Start OSC: Address already in use` ([VRCX LogWatcher.cs](https://github.com/vrcx-team/VRCX/blob/master/Dotnet/LogWatcher.cs)). Timestamps are only to the second, and join lines carry other users' display names and IDs ([VRChat feedback](https://feedback.vrchat.com/feature-requests/p/provide-userids-in-output-logs)), so a recorder should keep event types and drop the names or replace them with made-up ones (Astra's choice, below). The format is reverse-engineered and can change with any update.

Reactions are usually cut into neutral, onset, peak and fade phases ([Koelstra et al. 2010](https://www.ibug.doc.ic.ac.uk/media/uploads/documents/PAMI-2010-KoelstraEtAl-CAMERA.pdf); search summary). A simple version starts a reaction when her face changes sharply after a calm spell and ends it when the face settles. **No published minimum amount of data for a one-person reaction model was found.** REACT scores reactions on correlation, distance, diversity, realism and synchrony ([CaReDiff](https://huggingface.co/IVPL/CaReDiff); search summary), and "Turing tests" for nonverbal behaviour exist, such as a gaze test with a responsive virtual character ([Pfeiffer et al. 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3212571/)). The most useful test for Astra is a blind one: show friends, with their consent, short clips of Astra and of AI Astra reacting to the same replayed moment and ask which is which. Astra never reacts the same way twice either, so the fair yardstick is how consistent she is with herself, the way Park's team scored people against their own second answers.

### VRChat's rules and other people's consent

VRChat's Terms forbid "any robot, spambot, spider, crawler, scraper, or other automated means or interface not provided by us" ([VRChat Terms](https://hello.vrchat.com/legal); search summary). OSC *is* VRChat's own interface, so driving Astra's own avatar through it reads as allowed, while a separate always-on AI account, API scraping or harvesting other users' data is where the clause bites. That is a reading of the text, not a VRChat ruling: **this research found no official VRChat rule that allows or forbids an AI-controlled avatar**. VRChat's October 2026 statement on AI covers content such as thumbnails and treats personal-use tools as acceptable ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/vr-chat-community-protests-an-influx-of-ai-slop-onto-platform-hurting-discoverability-2000742109); search summary; one outlet dates it 28 October, [Mixed News](https://mixed-news.com/en/?p=38727)). One seller of an AI NPC kit advertises "no OSC, no bot accounts", which suggests sellers see bot accounts as a risk ([BOOTH](https://booth.pm/en/items/8822858); vendor claim, search summary).

Recording other people is governed by law, not by VRChat. About a dozen US states, including California and Illinois, require every party's consent to record a private conversation ([Metaverse Law](https://www.metaverselaw.com/two-party-consent-requirements-for-recording-calls/); [Rev](https://www.rev.com/blog/phone-call-recording-laws-state); search summary). Under the EU's GDPR, facial-expression data is not automatically "special category" biometric data unless it is used to identify someone ([arXiv 2309.10776](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.10776); search summary). Motion alone identified more than 50,000 VR users in a Berkeley study (see the AI characters section), so her face logs should be treated as biometric anyway. The lowest-risk design records only her own face and her own markers, keeps other people as roles ("friend A", "stranger") or made-up names, stays on her PC and out of git, and records anyone else's voice only with their explicit consent. Astra chose made-up names on 2026-11-03: "just change the names so we know it's the same person, but not use their actual name". The standard term is **pseudonymization**: one consistent pseudonym per person, never the real name. The face recorder now does this by default, turning each player VRChat's log names into `Player-` plus 6 hex digits from a keyed hash (HMAC-SHA256) with her PC's own salt, so the same person gets the same name in every session and no real name or user ID is written. A pseudonym is not anonymity: someone with the salt file and a guess at a user ID could check the guess, so the salt stays with the recordings. A visible note that AI Astra is an AI would match where VRChat's labelling debate is heading, though no rule requires it. This is general information, not legal advice.

## The Face Codec turns Unified Expressions into face frames and reaction clips

Astra asked what to call "our face language". On 2026-11-03 she chose **the Face Codec** ("Face codec is fine"). She asked for developer-minded names that describe the whole face, not creative ones, so the first working names and the creative alternatives were turned down. A codec is an encoder plus a decoder, the word software already uses for video and audio formats, and that is exactly the job: the Face Codec **encodes** Astra's whole face into a few plain values called **signals**, and **decodes** signals back into Unified Expressions weights. Its units get plain names too: a **face frame** is one moment, and a **reaction clip** is one reaction over a few seconds.

The Face Codec is deliberately **a layer on top of VRCFaceTracking's Unified Expressions, not a new standard**, because that standard has already won. VRCFaceTracking's code defines 88 Unified Expressions base shapes, from EyeSquintRight to NeckFlexLeft, plus 8 simple shapes such as MouthSmileLeft ([UnifiedExpressions.cs](https://github.com/benaclejames/VRCFaceTracking/blob/e3f40dd1fdd5e03a645a32691cbf54b4a99afbdd/VRCFaceTracking.Core/Params/Expressions/UnifiedExpressions.cs); [UnifiedSimpleExpressions.cs](https://github.com/benaclejames/VRCFaceTracking/blob/e3f40dd1fdd5e03a645a32691cbf54b4a99afbdd/VRCFaceTracking.Core/Params/Expressions/UnifiedSimpleExpressions.cs)). VRChat's native face tracking takes a single **102-byte blob at `/tracking/face/v1`**: 14 eye keys, then the same 88 shapes, one byte each ([VRChat creator docs, face-tracking/osc.md](https://github.com/vrchat-community/creator-docs/blob/5d21ba35600858a2c46a27da4791569f1745faca/Docs/docs/avatars/face-tracking/osc.md)). The research compared the two lists by script and found **VRChat's order for keys 14 to 101 identical to VRCFaceTracking's**. A rival standard would split a community that has just agreed, and would break tools such as PawlygonStudio's avatar matcher that already target Unified Expressions.

What the Face Codec adds is a small vocabulary a mind can read. The face-codec tool's encoder computes 13 plain face values, the signals (smile, frown, brow raise, brow lower, eyes wide, eyes closed, squint, jaw open, pucker, cheek puff, tongue out, and gaze left-right and up-down), from the exact names VRCFaceTracking and VRChat use. It turns them into words such as "smiling a lot, eyebrows raised, looking left" for AI Astra's model. **It never names an emotion**, which follows the science and law in the AI characters section below: a smile weight is a measurement, and "she is happy" is a guess. Its decoder keeps only the **face and eye shapes**, the 102 that VRChat's native face tracking carries, because Astra decided on 2026-11-03 that face-out may change only the Unified Expressions face shapes, the eyes and gaze: no toggles, no outfits (decision 7 of The Astral Plane, see the next section).

| Word | What it means | Built from |
|---|---|---|
| Signal | One plain face value, such as smile or brow raise (0 to 1; gaze −1 to 1) | Weighted Unified Expressions values, VRCFaceTracking's `v2/` avatar parameters and eye angles |
| Face frame | One moment: all 13 signals at once | One frame of face data |
| Reaction clip | One reaction over time, about 0.5 to 3 s | A run of face frames around a moment she marked or a VRChat event |
| Reaction book | Her reaction clips, the words that describe their moments, and her ratings: the heart of VRChat AI Astra, the copy of her | Her recorded sessions, kept on her PC only |

Three details about the data matter for recording. VRCFaceTracking sends each shape as an avatar parameter named `v2/<Name>` under whatever prefix the avatar uses, such as `FT/v2/JawOpen` ([BaseParameter.cs](https://github.com/benaclejames/VRCFaceTracking/blob/e3f40dd1fdd5e03a645a32691cbf54b4a99afbdd/VRCFaceTracking.Core/OSC/DataTypes/BaseParameter.cs); [VRCFaceTracking docs](https://github.com/VRCFaceTracking/docs/blob/3036f4668322f2364a918a885dc3a72c828359f3/docs/tutorial-avatars/tutorial-avatars-extras/parameters/parameters.mdx)), and combined parameters such as `v2/SmileFrown` and `v2/BrowExpression` are fixed formulas over the raw shapes ([UnifiedExpressionsParameters.cs](https://github.com/benaclejames/VRCFaceTracking/blob/e3f40dd1fdd5e03a645a32691cbf54b4a99afbdd/VRCFaceTracking.Core/Params/Expressions/UnifiedExpressionsParameters.cs)). **When an avatar uses VRChat's native face tracking, VRCFaceTracking stops sending `v2/` parameters and sends only the blob** ([BaseParameter.cs](https://github.com/benaclejames/VRCFaceTracking/blob/e3f40dd1fdd5e03a645a32691cbf54b4a99afbdd/VRCFaceTracking.Core/OSC/DataTypes/BaseParameter.cs)). And values read back from avatar parameters are coarser than the blob, because synced floats have 1/127 precision inside a 256-bit budget ([VRChat creator docs, animator parameters](https://github.com/vrchat-community/creator-docs/blob/5d21ba35600858a2c46a27da4791569f1745faca/Docs/docs/avatars/animator-parameters/index.md)). The Face Codec also has an art life: one face frame can be written onto real qubits, as the tenth experiment of the Quantum 10.

## Most OSC trouble is a port fight or a stale avatar config

Astra remembered downloading an OSC manager once to fix an OSC problem she still has. Such tools exist, and the reason they exist explains most OSC problems. VRChat **listens on UDP port 9000 and sends to port 9001** by default ([VRChat OSC Overview](https://docs.vrchat.com/docs/osc-overview); search summary), and VRCFaceTracking's defaults match ([OscTarget.cs](https://github.com/benaclejames/VRCFaceTracking/blob/e3f40dd1fdd5e03a645a32691cbf54b4a99afbdd/VRCFaceTracking.Core/Models/OscTarget.cs)). Only one program can hold a UDP port. VRChat could once send to only one app, so routers were built to copy its output to several ([VOR README](https://github.com/SutekhVRC/VOR)). Since **VRChat 2023.3.1 (August 2023)**, apps that speak **OSCQuery** announce themselves on the local network over mDNS, and VRChat opens a separate send stream to each one ([VRChat 2023.3.1 notes](https://docs.vrchat.com/docs/vrchat-202331); search summary). OSCQuery itself neither sends nor receives OSC; it only lets apps find each other ([vrc-oscquery-lib](https://github.com/vrchat-community/vrc-oscquery-lib/blob/095d0c98a88b12bbd6787c4bc5bec159f59bfca6/getting-started.md)). Current VRCFaceTracking uses it and no longer needs 9001 ([OscQueryService.cs](https://github.com/benaclejames/VRCFaceTracking/blob/e3f40dd1fdd5e03a645a32691cbf54b4a99afbdd/VRCFaceTracking.Core/Services/OscQueryService.cs)). In 2026 a router is needed only for older apps that insist on 9001.

### Her problem: many apps can send, only one can listen

Astra later described her problem (2026-11-03): "the overlap between OSC goes burr and VRC face tracking, and about how the porting is can send out a lot, but only can receive one at a time." That is the port rule above, exactly. Many apps can send to VRChat's input, port 9000 on her PC, at the same time, but only one app can listen on VRChat's output, port 9001. VRCFaceTracking, OSCGoesBrrr and now the face recorder all want to hear VRChat. Current VRCFaceTracking steps out of the way through OSCQuery, as above; whether OSCGoesBrrr supports OSCQuery was **not verified**; and the face recorder is a plain listener on 9001 today. So the fix is one of two: **OSCQuery** in every app that has it, or a **router** such as VRCOSC's, which holds 9001 once and hands each app its own port ([VRCOSC router wiki](https://github.com/VolcanicArts/VRCOSC/wiki/VRCOSC-Router); [VOR README](https://github.com/SutekhVRC/VOR)). To see which program holds the port now, she can run `netstat -ano | findstr :9001` and look up the PID in Task Manager (step 4 of the checklist below). This is a step on her PC, and step 0 of her roadmap.

The manager she downloaded was most likely VRCOSC or VOR, the two tools the guides point to for port conflicts, though only she can confirm. Common failures fall into four groups ([VRCOSC FAQ](https://github.com/VolcanicArts/VRCOSC/wiki/FAQ); [VRCFaceTracking docs](https://github.com/VRCFaceTracking/docs/blob/3036f4668322f2364a918a885dc3a72c828359f3/docs/vrcft-software/vrcft.mdx)): OSC is switched off; two apps want the same port ("address already in use"); a **stale per-avatar OSC config file**, which VRChat never updates by itself; or a firewall, VPN or extra network adapter blocking local traffic or mDNS. 2026 adds two more: avatars that use native face tracking get no `v2/` parameters by design, and VRCFaceTracking does not support local test avatars ([Resources.Designer.cs](https://github.com/benaclejames/VRCFaceTracking/blob/e3f40dd1fdd5e03a645a32691cbf54b4a99afbdd/VRCFaceTracking/Strings/Resources.Designer.cs)). Her description points at the port fight, so step 4 comes first for her; steps 2 and 4 together separate the two likeliest causes in a couple of minutes. The `netstat` command is a standard Windows tool, not from the sources.

Running AI Astra on a second VRChat account on the same PC needs care. Each client needs its own login through `--profile` ([VRChat wiki launch options](https://wiki.vrchat.com/wiki/Launch_Options); search summary) and its own ports through `--osc=inPort:outIP:outPort` ([VRChat launch options](https://docs.vrchat.com/docs/launch-options); search summary), for example `--profile=1` with in-port 9010 and out-port 9011, or both clients will try to take 9000. AI Astra's connector must then aim at the second client's in-port; aimed at 9000, it would move Astra's own avatar. VRCFaceTracking locks onto the first VRChat client it hears ([OscQueryService.cs](https://github.com/benaclejames/VRCFaceTracking/blob/e3f40dd1fdd5e03a645a32691cbf54b4a99afbdd/VRCFaceTracking.Core/Services/OscQueryService.cs)), so starting Astra's client and VRCFaceTracking before AI Astra's client should keep her face on her own avatar; that is read from the code, not tested. A VRChat bug report describes OSC config trouble with two profiles in one instance ([Canny 1275](https://feedback.vrchat.com/bug-reports/p/1275-reset-osc-does-not-work-have-to-delete-the-file-manually); search summary), and VRChat's rules on running two accounts at once were not checked.

**OSC Sonar** (the OSC Sealion, repo osc-sonar), Astra's planned pet for finding problems when several apps share OSC, would automate this checklist: see which process holds 9000 and 9001, list who announces themselves over mDNS, compare the avatar's config file with what OSCQuery reports, and watch the logs for "address already in use". Its core is the same "OSC tap" the face recorder needs. There are three ways to read face data without breaking anything ([VOR](https://github.com/SutekhVRC/VOR); [BinaryFaceDataSender.cs](https://github.com/benaclejames/VRCFaceTracking/blob/e3f40dd1fdd5e03a645a32691cbf54b4a99afbdd/VRCFaceTracking.Core/Services/BinaryFaceDataSender.cs); [OscSendService.cs](https://github.com/benaclejames/VRCFaceTracking/blob/e3f40dd1fdd5e03a645a32691cbf54b4a99afbdd/VRCFaceTracking.Core/Services/OscSendService.cs)):

| Way to read the face | Sees the full 102-key face? | Works on native face-tracking avatars? | Risk to her live face | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| OSCQuery receiver on VRChat's output | No, only the avatar's `v2/` values | No face values, only status | None | Moderate (mDNS plus a small web server; VRCFaceTracking's own mDNS code is 683 lines of C#, [mDNS folder](https://github.com/benaclejames/VRCFaceTracking/tree/e3f40dd1fdd5e03a645a32691cbf54b4a99afbdd/VRCFaceTracking.Core/mDNS)) |
| Own port through a router (VOR, VRCOSC) | No, routers carry VRChat-to-app traffic only | No | Low | Easy, but adds an app |
| Pass-through relay: VRCFaceTracking to relay to VRChat | Yes | Yes | Medium: if the relay stops, her face freezes until VRCFaceTracking points back at 9000 | Easy |

The face recorder today is a plain listener on VRChat's output port 9001, like an older app. On Windows it refuses to share or steal a port; if another app already holds 9001, it stops, says that only one app can listen there, and names the usual suspects (an OSC router, OSCLeash with OSCQuery off) and the `netstat` check. The relay is there as an option for native-face-tracking avatars, and an OSCQuery receiver is not built yet.

## VRChat's October 2026 launch made Astra's face data the standard

The open-source stack Astra uses is maintained by a small group of people who can be named. **VRCFaceTracking** was created by and belongs to **benaclejames**. It is Apache-2.0 licensed and had 917 stars and 1,220 commits on 3 November 2026 ([GitHub](https://github.com/benaclejames/VRCFaceTracking)). Release notes credit hyblocker, RantiMess and kusomaigo, plus translators P4tr0ll, stlenx, nkotech and VirtualMarten ([VRCFT releases](https://github.com/benaclejames/VRCFaceTracking/releases)). Earlier releases credited dfgHiatus and regzo2 ([VRCFT v4.0.0](https://github.com/benaclejames/VRCFaceTracking/releases/tag/v4.0.0)). VRCFT now ships on Steam ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3329480/VRCFaceTracking/)), where average monthly players rose from 725 to 1,137 between January and April 2026 ([stmstat](https://stmstat.com/pt/app/3329480)). The VRCFaceTracking GitHub organisation hosts **VRCFTModuleExample**, the starting template for anyone writing a hardware module ([GitHub org](https://github.com/VRCFaceTracking)). A 2026 fork by **RealWhyKnot** (v2026.9.18.0, 18 September 2026) adds Windows, Linux and macOS builds and multi-module tracking, with contributions from Happyrobot33, lumeyisnotyou and yuki-maruyama ([release](https://github.com/RealWhyKnot/VRCFaceTracking/releases/tag/v2026.9.18.0)). Whether benaclejames endorses the fork is unconfirmed.

The biggest change landed in the past two weeks. **VRChat entered an open beta of native face tracking on 23 October 2026** ([UploadVR](https://www.uploadvr.com/vrchat-gets-native-face-tracking-support-in-open-beta/)), and the feature went live in client **2026.4.1 on 28 October** ([VRChat on X](https://x.com/VRChat/status/2115512628550205783)). It is built on VRCFT's **Unified Expressions** standard. External apps feed it over OSC at `/tracking/face/v1` as **one 102-byte blob: 14 eye keys, then the 88 Unified Expressions shapes in VRCFT's own order** ([VRChat creator docs, face-tracking/osc.md](https://github.com/vrchat-community/creator-docs/blob/5d21ba35600858a2c46a27da4791569f1745faca/Docs/docs/avatars/face-tracking/osc.md)). Avatars SDK **3.10.6, dated 30 October 2026** in VRChat's docs source, brings the avatar side, and synced face data no longer counts against the avatar's parameter budget ([release 3.10.6 source](https://github.com/vrchat-community/creator-docs/blob/5d21ba35600858a2c46a27da4791569f1745faca/Docs/releases/release-3.10.6.md); [VRChat SDK 3.10.6](https://creators.vrchat.com/releases/release-3-10-6/)). A webcam mode called Selfie Expression covers desktop and Android and also drives Unified Expressions ([VRChat Creators: Face Tracking](https://creators.vrchat.com/avatars/face-tracking/); [face-tracking/index.md](https://github.com/vrchat-community/creator-docs/blob/5d21ba35600858a2c46a27da4791569f1745faca/Docs/docs/avatars/face-tracking/index.md)). The patch notes say the client syncs all 102 Unified Expressions values ([VRChat 2026.4.1](https://docs.vrchat.com/docs/vrchat-202641)). The blob layout and the SDK date were later read from VRChat's creator-docs source on GitHub; the client patch notes still rest on a search summary. One side effect matters for her rigs: **on an avatar that uses native face tracking, VRCFT stops sending its `v2/` avatar parameters and sends only the blob** ([BaseParameter.cs](https://github.com/benaclejames/VRCFaceTracking/blob/e3f40dd1fdd5e03a645a32691cbf54b4a99afbdd/VRCFaceTracking.Core/OSC/DataTypes/BaseParameter.cs)). What this means for Astra: VRCFT is no longer the only route into VRChat. It becomes one OSC source among several, so rigs and face-transfer tools should target Unified Expressions names plus ARKit aliases. **PawlygonStudio's** open Unity tool already does this matching ([PawlygonStudio README](https://github.com/PawlygonStudio/VRC-Facetracking/blob/main/README.md)). Tools that help existing avatars move to the native system are a fresh, open opportunity.

The do-it-yourself hardware scene is gathering around **Project Babble**. Its Baballonia app does "cross-platform, hardware-agnostic XR eye and face tracking" for VRChat, Resonite and ChilloutVR ([GitHub](https://github.com/Project-Babble/Baballonia); [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4091970/Project_Babble_Baballonia/)). On 26 September 2026 Babble announced a two-camera mouth tracker for Valve's Steam Frame ([hashout.jp](https://hashout.jp/gadget/11472/)). **EyeTrackVR** (1.2k stars) now runs standalone on the Steam Frame and has moved to the Babble Software Distribution License. Its ESP32 firmware, OpenIris, is by **lorow** ([GitHub](https://github.com/EyeTrackVR/EyeTrackVR)). The same handles turn up across these projects, dfgHiatus among them ([Steambase](https://steambase.io/games/project-babble-baballonia/info)). In practice this makes it one small, reachable community. Hardware is part of Astra's own story here. **Meta discontinued the Quest Pro in September 2024, stopped selling it in January 2025, and sells no face- or eye-tracking headset today** ([Road to VR](https://roadtovr.com/meta-discontinues-quest-pro/)). In late 2026 the main headset with built-in face tracking is **Samsung Galaxy XR**. It reports blendshape weights through Google's `XR_ANDROID_face_tracking` extension ([Android XR](https://developer.android.com/develop/xr/openxr/extensions/XR_ANDROID_face_tracking?hl=de)) and reaches PC VRChat through Virtual Desktop plus VRCFT ([Spacebar](https://www.spacebar.news/samsung-galaxy-xr-pc-vr-vrchat-guide/)). The alternative is an eye-tracking headset plus an add-on mouth tracker, such as HTC's **$119 Facial Tracker (38 blendshapes at 60 Hz)** ([VIVE shop](https://shop-us.vive.com/products/21045006)). Converting between Quest Pro and Galaxy XR expression sets is just the job her face-transfer tooling could do.

The VTuber world runs on the same buses (OSC and the VMC protocol), and its tools are made by long-running solo developers. **Emiliana (emilianavt)** builds VSeeFace and the open OpenSeeFace webcam tracker, and maintains a VTuber software list last updated in October 2026 ([gist](https://gist.github.com/emilianavt/cbf4d6de6f7fb01a42d4cce922795794)). The others include DenchiSoft (VTube Studio), Suvidriel (VNyan) and malaybaku (VMagicMirror) ([VNyan](https://suvidriel.itch.io/vnyan); [VMagicMirror](https://malaybaku.github.io/VMagicMirror/en/index)). Research supports the premise behind all of this work. Stanford VHIL's study of more than 200 people found that **facial expressions contributed more than body movement to conversational outcomes** in avatar conversations ([Kruzic, Kruzic, Herrera & Bailenson 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-76672-4)). A January 2026 preprint links avatar expressions to inter-brain synchrony, though it has not been peer reviewed ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.01.28.702209v1.full)). The effect is not universal. One IEEE TVCG study found that adding an avatar head did not raise social presence but the amount of facial expression did ([TVCG 2024](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1109/TVCG.2024.3372051)), and a JCMC study found no effect ([JCMC](https://academic.oup.com/jcmc/article/31/5/zmag021/8832613)). Expression intensity and avatar style appear to matter, which suits someone who builds stylised avatars.

## Labs now pipe headset blendshapes into photoreal heads and robot faces

Big-company avatar research has moved to **Gaussian-splat** heads. Meta's Codec Avatars Lab in Pittsburgh, where Shunsuke Saito keeps a publication list ([shunsukesaito.github.io](https://shunsukesaito.github.io/)), showed **URAvatar** at SIGGRAPH Asia 2024. URAvatar is a universal model trained on hundreds of light-stage scans, which is then tuned into a personal, relightable avatar from **a phone scan** ([project page](https://junxuan-li.github.io/urgca-website/)). Apple confirmed in October 2025 that Vision Pro Personas use Gaussian splatting ([Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/apple-confirms-personas-use-gaussian-splatting)). The academic paper closest to Astra's work is **OFERA** (IEEE VR 2026) from **Woontack Woo's KAIST lab**. It drives photoreal Gaussian heads in real time from **commercial headset blendshape signals**, the same kind of data she gets from VRCFT ([arXiv 2602.01748](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01748)). NVIDIA open-sourced **Audio2Face and Audio2Emotion** in September 2025, including the framework for training them on custom characters ([GitHub](https://github.com/NVIDIA/Audio2Face-3D-SDK)). That gives a speech-driven backup for mouth shapes on headsets that track only the eyes.

Physical robot faces have followed a clear path at **Columbia's Creative Machines Lab (Hod Lipson, Yuhang Hu)**. **EVA** (2021) mirrored a person's expressions using about **$900 in parts** ([Creative Machines Lab](https://www.creativemachineslab.com/eva.html)). **Emo** (Science Robotics, 2024) uses 26 actuators under silicone skin, predicts a coming smile **about 839 ms before it happens**, and smiles at the same moment as the person, because delayed mimicry reads as insincere ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2024/03/240327154854.htm); [Columbia](https://www.engineering.columbia.edu/about/news/robot-can-you-say-cheese)). In January 2026 a Science Robotics paper described a face robot that first learned what its 26 motors do **by watching itself in a mirror**, then learned human lip motion from YouTube video ([Columbia](https://www.engineering.columbia.edu/about/news/robot-learns-lip-sync)). Several secondary sources say Hu founded the Shanghai face-robot startup AheadForm ([Mike Kalil](https://mikekalil.com/blog/aheadform-elf-robot/)), whose Origin M1 head uses about 25 micro motors ([Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/technology/robotics/chinese-company-develops-creepy-ultra-lifelike-robot-face-watch-it-blink-twitch-and-nod)). Columbia's mirror step is the same calibration problem Astra would face in mapping VRCFT parameters onto servos.

Several teams already run her exact data path into hardware. **Honda Research Institute Europe** (Chao Wang, Michael Gienger, Fan Zhang) uses a **Unity app on a Meta Quest Pro** to teleoperate a robot with LED eyes, moving ears, a neck and arms. Of the Quest Pro's roughly 70 blendshapes, the team uses only **the 7 that most affect perceived emotion**, and a learned model trained on these demonstrations then generates expressions on its own in real time ([arXiv 2508.08999](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08999v2)). Japan's **Yui** android uses the HTC Facial Tracker to drive 28 facial actuation points ([arXiv 2312.06310](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.06310v1)). A June 2026 paper describes a hand-tuned mapping from headset blendshape weights for its field deployment ([arXiv 2606.08099](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.08099)). The Italian Institute of Technology's **iCub3** (Daniele Pucci) retargets an operator's gaze, eyelids and mouth from about 400 km away ([arXiv 2203.06972](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.06972v2)). Sven Behnke's **NimbRo** team (Bonn) won the **$5M ANA Avatar XPRIZE** in 2022, partly by animating a photo of the operator's face from cameras inside the headset ([XPRIZE](https://go.xprize.org/news/ana-avatar-xprize-winners); [IROS 2022](https://www.ais.uni-bonn.de/papers/IROS_2022_Rochow.pdf)). **ExFace** turns human blendshapes into motor commands at 60 fps within 0.15 s ([arXiv 2504.14477](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14477v1)). Tsinghua's **Morpheus** face publishes its hardware and code ([arXiv 2507.16645](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16645v1)), and **Rena** learns face-to-servo mapping by imitation, with 4.4% servo error ([arXiv 2510.23059](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23059v1)).

For a solo builder, a face-tracked head is realistic at **about $300 to $1,000 in parts**. A 2025 Fab Academy student project in Puebla came to about $300 for a 16-servo head with a Raspberry Pi 5 ([Fab Academy, Osorio](https://fabacademy.org/2025/labs/puebla/students/javier-osorio/finalproject.html); [Fab Academy, Vivar](https://fabacademy.org/2025/labs/puebla/students/erwin-vivar/final-project.html)). Maker Will Cogley's open animatronic eye designs are widely reused ([brainforest/robot-head](https://github.com/brainforest/robot-head); [Arduino Blog](https://blog.arduino.cc/2024/12/09/this-frighteningly-realistic-animatronic-head-features-expressive-silicone-skin/)). The InMoov project warns that cheap SG90 servos wear out quickly ([InMoov i2Head](https://inmoov.fr/i2head-for-inmoov/)). VRCFT's own documentation says what its parameters control "is completely up to you" ([GitHub](https://github.com/benaclejames/VRCFaceTracking)). Even so, no one has published a VRCFT-to-servo bridge. The build would be an OSC listener in Python feeding an Arduino or ESP32 with a PCA9685 driver and 8 to 12 servos for the eyes, lids, brows, jaw and mouth corners, calibrated with a "mirror" step and smoothed with a low-pass filter. A stylised or LED face sidesteps the uncanny valley. Viewers praised AheadForm's ultra-realistic head and also called it creepy, mostly for its blinking and fixed gaze ([Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/technology/robotics/chinese-company-develops-creepy-ultra-lifelike-robot-face-watch-it-blink-twitch-and-nod)). Commercial realistic heads cost **$150,000 to $175,000** (Realbotix Aria) ([Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/photo-story/meet-aria-humanoid-robot-girlfriend)).

## AI characters mostly hear voices, which leaves the face-reading pet unclaimed

Most "emotional" AI character platforms read the user's **voice, not the face**. Hume's EVI, Inworld's voice runtime and NVIDIA's Audio2Emotion all work from speech, and this research found no evidence that EVI or Inworld take facial input ([Hume products](https://www.hume.ai/products); [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/08/13/3132320/0/en/inworld-runtime-the-first-ai-runtime-for-consumer-applications.html); [NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/nvidia-audio2face-is-now-open-source/345978)). Convai's "vision" feature sees the game scene from the character's point of view, not the player's webcam ([Convai docs](https://docs.convai.com/api-docs/plugins-and-integrations/convai-unreal-engine-plugin/features/vision)). Hume shut down its original face-and-voice Expression Measurement API on 14 June 2026 ([Hume FAQ](https://dev.hume.ai/docs/expression-measurement/faq)) and opened a limited-release successor for live video on 6 October 2026 ([Hume changelog](https://dev.hume.ai/expression-measurement/changelog/2026/10/6.md)). In January 2026, Google DeepMind licensed Hume's technology and hired its CEO Alan Cowen ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/22/google-reportedly-snags-up-team-behind-ai-voice-startup-hume-ai)). The products that do watch faces are video-call avatars and hardware companions. Tavus's Raven models track the user's eye contact and expressions during calls ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/03/06/tavus-introduces-family-ai-models-power-real-time-human-face-face-interaction/)). **Razer's Project AVA**, shown at CES 2026, is a holographic desk companion with an HD camera and eye tracking ([Razer](https://www.razer.com/newsroom/product-news/project-ava/)). It is the closest commercial match to her desktop pets. Soul Machines, the best-known "digital human" firm, went into receivership in February 2026 and was bought by AppDirect in September ([NBR](https://www.nbr.co.nz/tech/soul-machines-in-receivership); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/09/28/appdirect-acquires-interactive-ai-avatar-firm-soul-machines-for-advisors-and-businesses/)).

The open-source scene is closer to her than any company. **Open-LLM-VTuber** is an offline Live2D companion with camera input, a transparent **desktop pet mode** and Claude support ([GitHub](https://github.com/Open-LLM-VTuber/Open-LLM-VTuber)). **Project AIRI** (moeru-ai) is a "soul container" with an Electron "Tamagotchi" desktop build and about 35k stars ([GitHub](https://github.com/moeru-ai/airi); [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/project-airi/about)). For VRChat specifically, **uezo's aiavatarkit** drives avatar facial expressions from an LLM over OSC ([SourcePulse](https://www.sourcepulse.org/projects/1833526)), and **Krekun's vrchat-mcp-osc** lets Claude Desktop control a VRChat avatar ([mcpservers.org](https://mcpservers.org/servers/Krekun/vrchat-mcp-osc)). Researchers have also put a GPT-4 NPC with matching facial expressions inside VRChat ([Nweon](https://paper.nweon.com/?p=15770)). All of these send expression *out* to an avatar. None of the projects found takes the user's VRCFT face data *in* and uses it to set an AI character's mood. Astra's stack already produces that missing signal, so linking her face-in pipeline to an OSC face-out bridge would create a two-way loop that no one appears to have published. Meta's open **Seamless Interaction** models are the nearest research. They generate a listener's facial reactions from the other person's face and voice, and can drive 3D avatars ([Meta](https://ai.meta.com/research/seamless-interaction/)).

Both the science and the law point the same design direction: react to facial signals, and don't announce an emotion. The key review by Barrett and colleagues (2019) concluded that a facial configuration such as a scowl often signals something other than a specific emotion ([APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/emotional-expressions-reconsidered-challenges-to-inferring-emotion-from-human-facial-movements.html)). Microsoft retired Azure's emotion inference in 2022, citing the lack of scientific consensus on defining emotions ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2022/06/21/microsoft-restricts-access-controversial-ai-facial-recognition-technology/)). Since **2 February 2025, the EU AI Act bans emotion recognition from biometric data in workplaces and schools** ([Future of Privacy Forum](https://fpf.org/blog/red-lines-under-eu-ai-act-unpacking-the-prohibition-of-emotion-recognition-in-the-workplace-and-education-institutions/)). The Commission's guidelines say that detecting a smile does not count as emotion recognition ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/02/17/understanding-the-eu-ai-acts-prohibited-practices-key-workplace-and-advertising-102k011)). A home hobby project falls outside the ban, though a distributed pet that labels users' emotions could carry transparency duties (this is an inference, not legal advice). Shipped games already work this way. *Before Your Eyes* advances its story on real blinks ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Before_Your_Eyes)), and *Nevermind* adapts its horror to heart rate and webcam signs of distress ([Affectiva](https://www.affectiva.com/success-story/flying-mollusk/)). So a pet should respond to raw weights such as a smile, a lowered brow, blinks and gaze. Privacy matters too. Vivek Nair's Berkeley team **identified more than 50,000 VR users from head and hand motion alone** ([Berkeley EECS](https://eecs.berkeley.edu/news/eecs-researchers-explore-unprecedented-privacy-risks-vr/)), so Astra's logged face streams should be treated as biometric data. Sending only numbers, never images, to an LLM, as the hobby project EmoChat does with labels, is the safe pattern ([EmoChat](https://www.mintlify.com/Dani-zm/emotion-recognition-ai/quickstart)).

## Her PC already makes unpredictable bits; quantum bits add a story

Astra asked why she would need truly random quantum bits to vary AI Astra's small mood shifts, and whether we can't find random data now. **We can, and her PC already does.** There are four kinds of random generator:

| Kind | Example | Can anyone predict it? | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pseudorandom (PRNG) | Python's `random` (the Mersenne Twister), which "should not be used for security purposes" | Yes, with enough of its output; it can also be replayed from a seed | Games, animation, simulation ([CPython random docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/python/cpython/main/Doc/library/random.rst)) |
| Cryptographically secure (CSPRNG) | Python's `secrets` and `os.urandom`, which use Windows' `BCryptGenRandom` | Not in practice | Keys, passwords, anything ([CPython os docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/python/cpython/main/Doc/library/os.rst)) |
| Hardware true random | Intel and AMD chips' RDRAND and RDSEED, which sample thermal noise in the silicon | No | Seeding the generators above ([arXiv 2009.03777](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.03777); search summary) |
| Quantum random (QRNG) | A measurement of a quantum process, such as a qubit after a Hadamard gate | No, and physics explains why | Provenance, and certified randomness against a cheating device |

Free outside sources exist too. ANU's quantum service reportedly gives about 100 requests a month on a free key, with paid use through AWS ([packagist](https://packagist.org/packages/dkzr/anu-quantum-random-engine); [AWS Marketplace](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/B09SFWPDRJ); search summary). random.org uses atmospheric noise, with 1 million free bits per IP ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random.org); search summary). NIST's Randomness Beacon publishes 512 bits every 60 s that mix in a photon-detection quantum generator, with the warning "DO NOT USE BEACON GENERATED VALUES AS SECRET CRYPTOGRAPHIC KEYS", because everyone sees them ([NIST](https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/interoperable-randomness-beacons/beacon-20); search summary). drand, run by independent nodes, publishes a publicly verifiable value every 3 s ([drand README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/drand/drand/master/README.md)). Even Cloudflare's famous lava-lamp wall is mixed into the operating system's generator as an extra source, not a replacement ([Cloudflare](https://cloudflare.com/learning/ssl/lava-lamp-encryption); search summary).

Quantum randomness adds two real things. The first is **provenance**: AI Astra's small moods can honestly be "seeded by a quantum measurement". The second, at the research frontier, is **certified randomness**. In March 2025 Quantinuum's 56-qubit H2 and JPMorgan produced at least 71,313 bits proven fresh even if the quantum computer were malicious, checked with about 1.1 exaFLOPS of supercomputing ([JPMorgan](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/about/technology/blog/certified-randomness); search summary). That protection matters for lotteries and cryptography audits. A pet's mood has no adversary. **For moods there is no practical difference**: nobody watching AI Astra could tell quantum-seeded drift from drift seeded by `secrets`, or even by plain `random`, which Python's own docs file under "modelling and simulation" ([CPython secrets docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/python/cpython/main/Doc/library/secrets.rst)). A seeded generator even has an advantage: a mood trace can be replayed exactly, for debugging or for comparing AI Astra with Astra's recordings.

The quantum coin tool turns this into a fair experiment. Raw bits from IBM's qubits are **biased and correlated**, as a 43,560-bit test on the 20-qubit IBM Q 20 Tokyo showed ([IACR ePrint 2020/078](https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/078); search summary), so they need cleaning. The coin's fill script puts a Hadamard gate on each qubit, measures, and runs von Neumann debiasing on each qubit separately: look at pairs, drop 00 and 11, keep 01 as 0 and 10 as 1, which keeps at most a quarter of the bits ([arXiv 2301.11973](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.11973); search summary). By IBM's formula, a 156-qubit job of 10,000 shots costs about **5.5 s** of QPU time and gives 1.56 million raw bits ([IBM workload usage](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Qiskit/documentation/main/docs/guides/estimate-job-run-time.ipynb)), so at most about 390,000 clean ones. The bits go into a **coin jar** file on her PC, and each is used once. When the jar is empty the coin falls back to her PC's `secrets` and labels every draw by its source. Each reaction choice takes 64 bits to seed a local generator, because a mood updated 10 times a second on two dimensions needs about 72,000 random draws an hour (about 4.6 million bits at 64 bits each). That is more than the free quotas could keep up with for daily use: IBM's 10 minutes, at about 390,000 clean bits per 5.5 s job, would cover only about 9 hours of direct draws every 28 days. And network calls do not belong in the VRChat loop. The fill script runs only with Astra's own IBM account and has never been run. **The jar is an experiment and a story; her PC's own random numbers are already enough.**

### Is that how a human is?

No, and the difference is the useful part. **People are bad random generators.** Asked to make random sequences, they switch too often and avoid streaks, and they judge sequences that alternate 60 to 70% of the time as "most random", when a fair coin alternates 50% ([Frontiers in Psychology 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01027/pdf); search summary). Yet people vary all the time, and their variation has **structure**. Response-time series often show **1/f ("pink") noise**, slow wandering plus fast jitter (Gilden, Thornton and Mallon, *Science* 1995: [ModelDB record](https://modeldb.science/citations/237744); search summary), though Farrell, Wagenmakers and Ratcliff argued it is not universal ([PubMed 17201378](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17201378); search summary). The gaps between blinks are skewed and roughly **log-normal**, at 13.7 ± 7.8 blinks a minute in one study ([Arq. Bras. Oftalmol. 2010](https://www.sidalc.net/search/Record/oai:scielo:S0004-27492010000400005); search summary). Psychologists model a person's mood as an **Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) process** that drifts but keeps being pulled back to a personal "home base", with its own pull strength and volatility ([Penn State QuantDev](https://quantdev.ssri.psu.edu/node/43100); [Kuppens et al. 2010](https://sites.socsci.uci.edu/~zoravecz/bayes/data/Articles/Kuppens2010FCAFI.pdf); search summary). Noise in the nervous system, from molecules to muscles, explains much of the trial-to-trial variation ([Faisal, Selen and Wolpert 2008](https://wolpertlab.neuroscience.columbia.edu/sites/default/files/content/papers/FaiSelWol08.pdf); search summary).

Is the brain quantum? Quantum-brain ideas remain **unproven minority hypotheses**. Tegmark estimated brain decoherence times of 10^-13 to 10^-20 s, far too short to matter for thought ([arXiv quant-ph/0005025](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0005025); search summary). An experiment under Gran Sasso ruled out the simplest gravity-collapse model behind Penrose and Hameroff's Orch-OR, which the team called "highly implausible" in that form ([Physics World](https://physicsworld.com/a/quantum-theory-of-consciousness-put-in-doubt-by-underground-experiment/); search summary). A 2024 rat study found that a microtubule drug delayed anaesthetic unconsciousness by 69 s on average, which fits quantum-microtubule models but does not demonstrate quantum effects ([eNeuro](https://www.eneuro.org/content/11/8/ENEURO.0291-24.2024); search summary). Fisher's idea of entangled "Posner molecules" as neural qubits still waits for evidence that the molecules exist in the body ([arXiv 1508.05929](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.05929); [review, arXiv 1910.08423](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.08423); search summary). The fair answer: a human is not a quantum coin. A person is a noisy, self-correlated system drifting around a home base. White noise from any source, quantum included, would make AI Astra's face look like it was twitching.

| Layer | Time scale | Human-shaped model | What to measure in her own recordings |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mood | Minutes | OU drift around a home base; events push it, the pull brings it back | Her home base, and how fast her face returns to neutral |
| Expression texture | 0.2 to 5 s | Small pink noise, clamped to allowed expressions | The slope of her micro-motion spectrum |
| Blinks and glances | Single events | Log-normal gaps, with a mean of about 4.4 s at 13.7 blinks a minute | Her own blink-gap distribution |

Animation got here first. Perlin and Goldberg's Improv system (SIGGRAPH 96) used noise at every level, "ranging from low-level animation triggering (e.g., eye blinking), to mid-level behaviors (e.g., approach/avoid), to high-level attitudes" ([NYU MRL](https://mrl.cs.nyu.edu/projects/improv/); search summary). The quantum coin already has mood drift, pink noise and blink timing with hard limits. Fitting their settings to Astra's own recordings, her personal **noise fingerprint**, is not built yet, and it is a stronger cloning signal than where the bits come from.

## Euler's formula is the grammar of every quantum gate, not the engine

The terms need sorting out first. **Euler's formula** is e^(iθ) = cos θ + i sin θ, for any angle θ. **Euler's identity**, e^(iπ) + 1 = 0, is one value of it: a half-turn, which multiplies by −1. Quantum computing uses the formula everywhere and the identity at specific, important moments. A quantum state lists one complex number, an *amplitude*, for each possible outcome. Euler's formula splits each amplitude into a length and an angle. The length squared is the probability, and the angle, the *phase* e^(iφ), cannot be seen in any single measurement. It decides how alternatives interfere. Picture each amplitude as an arrow. When two paths lead to the same outcome, their arrows add: arrows pointing the same way reinforce, and arrows a half-turn apart (differing by e^(iπ) = −1) cancel. The standard single-qubit form, |ψ⟩ = cos(θ/2)|0⟩ + e^(iφ) sin(θ/2)|1⟩, comes straight from Euler's formula, and the Bloch sphere picture is built on it ([arXiv 2503.19109](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.19109)).

Every quantum gate is an Euler rotation. All closed-system quantum dynamics is a matrix exponential, U = e^(−iHt/ħ), and "Hamiltonian simulation" is defined as building that U ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_simulation)). For the Pauli matrices, which square to the identity, the exponential is **literally Euler's formula with a matrix in place of the number i**: e^(iθA) = cos θ·I + i sin θ·A ([problem set](https://www.ilorentz.org/QI/problemset_linalg_QI.pdf)). IBM's Qiskit writes its rotation gates in exactly this e^(−iθP/2) form ([Qiskit RZZ docs](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/api/qiskit/0.31/qiskit.circuit.library.RZZGate)). The **Z gate is diag(1, e^(iπ))**, Euler's identity in the form of a gate, and turning a qubit a full 360° about Z gives −I, a factor of −1 again. 3Blue1Brown explains why e^(it) moves in a circle: a point whose velocity is always i times its position goes halfway round to −1 after π seconds ([3Blue1Brown](https://www.3blue1brown.com/lessons/eulers-formula-dynamically)). Schrödinger's equation for a state of definite energy is the same equation. So every energy component of a quantum system runs its own "Euler clock" at speed E/ħ, and everything measurable comes from the angles *between* the clock hands.

The identity itself sits at the heart of interference. The **Hadamard gate is the 2-point quantum Fourier transform**, H = (1/√2)[[1, 1], [1, e^(iπ)]]. The N-th roots of unity always sum to zero, and for N = 2 that sum *is* 1 + e^(iπ) = 0. So Euler's identity is exactly the cancellation that lets a Hadamard turn phase into a measurable bit. Larger QFTs use the entries e^(2πijk/N), and quantum phase estimation reads out the angle of an eigenvalue e^(2πiθ). These are the building blocks of Shor's algorithm and of physics simulation ([IBM Quantum Learning](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/learning/en/courses/fundamentals-of-quantum-algorithms/phase-estimation-and-factoring/phase-estimation-procedure)). The Hadamard test gives outcome 0 with probability cos²(φ/2), so when the phase is e^(iπ) the ancilla qubit reports Euler's −1 with certainty ([Qulacs](https://dojo.qulacs.org/en/latest/notebooks/2.2_Hadamard_test.html); [PennyLane](https://pennylane.ai/demos/tutorial_phase_kickback)). Simulation algorithms chain many small Euler rotations. Seth Lloyd proved in 1996 that this "Trotter" product approach efficiently simulates any *local* quantum system ([arXiv 1004.5528](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1004.5528)). A numerical check run for this research shows how the method behaves: for H = X + Z, the error falls from 0.80 with one step to 0.011 with 64 steps, roughly as 1/n. The smallest demo that makes the identity visible fits in a few lines. In the circuit H → P(θ) → H on |0⟩, the amplitude of outcome 0 is (1 + e^(iθ))/2, so at θ = π it equals (e^(iπ) + 1)/2 = 0, and outcome 1 becomes certain:

```python
# numpy, tested 2026-11-03: the "Euler slider"
import numpy as np
H = np.array([[1, 1], [1, -1]]) / np.sqrt(2)
for theta in np.linspace(0, 2*np.pi, 9):
    P = np.diag([1, np.exp(1j*theta)])            # phase gate = Euler's formula on |1>
    out = H @ P @ H @ np.array([1, 0])
    print(f"{theta/np.pi:.2f}π  amp0={(1+np.exp(1j*theta))/2:.3f}  P(1)={abs(out[1])**2:.3f}")
```

Calling Euler's identity "the foundation" goes too far in four ways. First, it is one value of a general formula and contains no physics. It doesn't say which Hamiltonian describes the universe, and the Hamiltonian is where all the physics lives. Second, it is not where the speedup comes from. Scott Aaronson notes that **real-number amplitudes typically suffice for quantum computing** ([Shtetl-Optimized](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=4021)). The power comes from an n-qubit state having 2^n amplitudes, the exponential bookkeeping that Feynman's 1982 paper pointed to ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02650179)). Third, whether nature *needs* complex numbers is an open debate. Experiments in 2022 ruled out real-number quantum theory under the standard rule for combining systems. One superconducting-qubit test scored 8.09 against a real-number limit of 7.66 ([Chen et al., PRL 2022](https://physics.aps.org/featured-article-pdf/10.1103/PhysRevLett.128.040403); [Wu et al., PRL 2022](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevLett.129.140401); [Renou et al. 2021](https://web3.arxiv.org/abs/2101.10873)). But a June 2026 PRL paper and a 2025 preprint built real-number theories with a different combining rule that predict the same results ([Barrios Hita et al.](https://nachrichten.idw-online.de/2026/06/19/physics-publication-in-physical-review-letters-does-quantum-mechanics-need-complex-numbers); [Hoffreumon & Woods](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02808v2)). The two results do not conflict. Complex numbers remain the natural, universal working language, and every SDK uses them. Fourth, the foundation also needs superposition and the tensor product, the Born rule, measurement and decoherence, the Hamiltonian, and error correction. A framing that stays accurate and keeps Euler at the centre: *"Every quantum gate is an Euler rotation, and e^(iπ) = −1 is the half-turn that makes alternatives cancel."* This research found **no project, paper or artwork that explicitly makes Euler's identity the organising core of quantum computing**. Bloch-sphere visualisers exist but never mention it ([FastHTML gallery](https://gallery.fastht.ml/vizualizations/bloch_sphere/display); [BlochSphereVisualizer](https://github.com/hmasum52/BlochSphereVisualizer)). The niche is open.

## Nobody simulates the universe; about 100 qubits run toy universes

Feynman's founding argument (1982) was that describing a quantum system on an ordinary computer takes memory that grows exponentially with the number of particles, so the simulator should itself be quantum ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02650179)). In game-engine terms: in a classical engine, adding a particle adds a few floats to the save file, while in an exact quantum simulation, adding one qubit-sized particle *doubles* it. Physicists do not apply this idea to "the universe". They apply it to specific model Hamiltonians, especially the lattice gauge theories behind electromagnetism and the strong force. In those theories, real-time dynamics and dense matter run into the "sign problem", where classical sampling blows up exponentially. Two roadmaps set out the field: Bauer, Davoudi and about 30 co-authors ([PRX Quantum 2023](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PRXQuantum.4.027001)), and the CERN/DESY/IBM working group, which says the qubit counts needed are "largely beyond" today's noisy devices ([Di Meglio et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.03236)).

| Group | Hardware | Size | What it simulated | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|

The table shows how big the toy universes are as of late 2026. Each step forward adds a dimension (1+1D to 2+1D), moves from simpler "Abelian" theories to the "non-Abelian" kind behind the strong force, or goes from static states to collisions. Work from 2025 and 2026 took all three steps in small form, and a September 2026 thesis reports what it calls the first numerical evidence of inelastic particle production in simulated collisions ([arXiv 2609.28825](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28825)). None of these simulations has been shown to beat classical computers at particle physics. The US Department of Energy says only that future simulations are *expected* to surpass classical computing ([DOE](https://www.energy.gov/science/np/articles/nuclear-physicists-create-scalable-quantum-circuits-simulate-fundamental)), even though some headlines claimed more ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2025/11/251118220104.htm)). The cosmology "universes" are analogs: sound waves in ultracold gas follow the same equations as quantum fields in expanding spacetime. Heidelberg's lab universe expanded **by a factor of less than 3, against the 10^26 that inflation theory assumes** ([Physics Today](https://pubs.aip.org/physicstoday/online/41858/The-early-universe-in-a-quantum-gas)), and gravity is never dynamical. The 2022 "wormhole" is the cautionary tale. Independent physicists argued that its machine-learned 9-qubit model showed artifacts of its own small size, not gravity ([arXiv 2308.00697](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.00697); [Woit](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~woit/wordpress/?p=13425)). The lesson for Astra is to check what is actually being simulated, not the headline.

Some limits do not depend on engineering. Seth Lloyd calculated that the observable universe could have performed at most **about 10^120 operations on about 10^90 bits** (about 10^120 bits if gravity is included). Lloyd framed these numbers as the *minimum* resources needed to simulate the universe on a quantum computer ([arXiv quant-ph/0110141](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0110141)). A computer built inside the universe has fewer degrees of freedom than the universe itself, so a full-resolution self-simulation is ruled out by counting before engineering even starts. Franco Vazza added energy costs in 2025. The paper concluded that even a low-resolution simulation of *Earth* would need energy that is "either incompatible with physics or astronomically large" ([Frontiers in Physics](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physics/articles/10.3389/fphy.2025.1561873/pdf)), though critics say "impossible" overstates it ([Lincoln Cannon](https://lincoln.metacannon.net/2025/05/vazza-overstates-constraints-on-simulation.html)). David Wolpert's computer-science result is purely logical. Nothing in logic forbids a universe from simulating itself; the binding limits are physical ([Mapping Ignorance](https://mappingignorance.org/2026/01/06/simulation-hypothesis/)). Two further facts stand even with a perfect machine: quantum mechanics forbids copying an unknown state, so the universe's present state could never be loaded in, and chaos would amplify any error. The people actually doing this work include Martin Savage's InQubator for Quantum Simulation, which posts its papers ([IQuS](https://iqus.uw.edu/publications/)); the CERN Quantum Technology Initiative ([arXiv 2511.03883](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.03883)); Fermilab's SQMS centre, renewed in November 2025 with **$125M over five years** ([Fermilab](https://news.fnal.gov/2025/11/fermilabs-sqms-center-funded-with-125-million-to-shape-the-future-of-quantum-information-science/)); and the Simons "It from Qubit" collaboration on spacetime and entanglement ([Simons Foundation](https://www.simonsfoundation.org/collaborations/it-from-qubit/)).

## Full Standard Model physics sits decades past IBM's 2029 machine

Error correction now works in the lab, but useful fault-tolerant machines are still promises. Google's Willow chip (December 2024) halved its logical error rate each time its code grew ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/research/google-willow-quantum-chip/)). Quantinuum's 98-qubit **Helios** launched with **48 error-corrected logical qubits** ([Quantinuum](https://www.quantinuum.com/products-solutions/quantinuum-systems/helios)) and reached a record logical error rate of about 5×10⁻⁶ per round in October 2026 ([HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/2026/10/19/quantinuum-says-helios-results-strengthen-its-path-to-apollo/)). Harvard and QuEra encoded **up to 96 logical qubits** in neutral atoms ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/neutral-atom-quantum-computing)). IBM promises **Starling in 2029 (200 logical qubits, 100 million gates)** and **Blue Jay in 2033 or later (2,000 logical qubits, 1 billion operations)** ([IBM](https://www.ibm.com/quantum/blog/large-scale-ftqc)). Dates slip, though. As of 22 October 2026 there was no public demonstration of IBM's 2026 Kookaburra chip ([Quantum Investor Notebook](https://www.quantuminvestornotebook.com/scorecards/ibm)), and IBM usually updates its roadmap in November, so this could change within weeks. Rigetti's 108-qubit system arrived months late ([The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/01/13/rigetti-delays-108-qubit-cepheus-system/)). Nature's editors said Microsoft's Majorana paper "does not represent evidence for Majorana zero modes" ([Science](https://www.science.org/content/article/debate-erupts-around-microsoft-s-blockbuster-quantum-computing-claims)). The best independent check is DARPA's Quantum Benchmarking Initiative, which asks whether any approach reaches utility scale **by 2033**. In October 2026 it moved Atom Computing, Diraq, IBM and IonQ into its final stage, joining Microsoft and PsiQuantum ([Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/darpa-selects-atom-computing-diraq-ibm-and-ionq-for-final-stage-c-verification-under-quantum-benchmarking-initiative/)).

|---|---|---|---|

| Microsoft / Atom Computing "Magne" ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/neutral-atom-quantum-computing)) | 50 | — | Promised for start of 2027 |

Estimates for the real target, 3+1D quantum chromodynamics (QCD, the theory of quarks and gluons), are falling fast but remain enormous. An improved method put the total at **1.7×10^27 T gates** ([arXiv 2503.11888](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.11888)). An August 2026 paper needs about **12·L³ logical qubits** for an L×L×L lattice ([arXiv 2608.17115](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.17115)). For the L = 32 lattices that QCD practitioners usually want, that is about 400,000 logical qubits. Assume an optimistic 10^6 to 10^9 logical T gates per second, and 10^27 gates take 10^18 to 10^21 seconds, longer than the universe's age of about 4×10^17 seconds. So even today's best estimate needs many more orders of magnitude of algorithmic improvement. Industry overviews expect early fault-tolerant demonstrations in **2028–2029** and commercially valuable machines in the **early to mid 2030s**, with "substantial uncertainty" ([The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/09/04/main-challenges-facing-quantum-computing/)). Aaronson, long a sceptic of hype, wrote in August 2026 that "the reality of quantum advantage is no longer a live question" but that usefulness still is ([Shtetl-Optimized](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=9979)). Gil Kalai still doubts that quantum computers can scale at all ([Kalai](https://gilkalai.wordpress.com/2026/03/10/scott-aaronsons-view-of-my-view-about-quantum-computing/)). My assessment from these sources, since no roadmap gives dates: beyond-classical results for 2+1D gauge theories and large 1+1D collision simulations could come between the late 2020s and early 2030s; small non-Abelian ("strong force"-like) dynamics in the 2030s; real QCD observables in the 2040s or later; and a full universe never. Waiting for hardware alone is a plan measured in decades, and no amount of personal money shortens it.

## Starting costs $0, and skill is the bottleneck, not money

Nobody will sell an individual a useful quantum computer, and nobody needs to. The only machines for sale are SpinQ's 2-qubit teaching devices, at about **$8,700** for the Gemini Mini ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/you-can-buy-a-portable-quantum-computer-for-under-dollar9k/)) and about **$44,000** for the Gemini Lab. That fits on a 24 GB card, matching community benchmarks ([blueqat](https://blueqat.com/yuichiro_minato2/74608c73-a292-462a-b10e-ad594283d04e)). Each added qubit doubles the memory. For the one- to ten-qubit Euler demos and small toy universes she would build first, her own PC beats a noisy cloud machine.

The free learning path that builds most directly on Euler's formula starts with 3Blue1Brown's e^(iπ) dynamics lesson ([3Blue1Brown](https://www.3blue1brown.com/lessons/eulers-formula-dynamically)), then moves to Quantum Country, Andy Matuschak and Michael Nielsen's spaced-repetition primer ([Quantum Zeitgeist](https://quantumzeitgeist.com/top-10-free-resources-for-quantum-computing/)), and then to running the Euler-slider and Hadamard-test demos above. The first toy universe is then within reach: Trotterised time evolution of an Ising chain, followed by the Schwinger model, on her own GPU, checked against the IQuS papers that ran the same model on 112 real qubits. The PennyLane Codebook and Microsoft's Quantum Katas supply drills along the way, Nielsen & Chuang is the standard reference ([book list](https://github.com/poig/Qc_book_list)), and John Watrous's 2025 text is a free alternative ([arXiv 2507.11536](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.11536)).

Her Unity and VR skills are her way into the quantum community, because the projects that fill the open Euler niche all run on classical simulators: a VR "Euler slider" whose dial makes the arrows 1 and e^(iθ) visibly cancel at π, "Euler clocks" for a two-level system, a Bloch-sphere view of Trotter steps converging on the exact path, or a desktop pet that flips its sign through a real H–Z–H circuit. The community is built for people without an institution. The **Unitary Foundation gives $4,000 microgrants** for open-source and educational quantum projects ([Unitary Foundation](https://unitary.foundation/grants)). QOSF runs a free mentorship program for "quantum enthusiasts" ([QOSF](https://qosf.org/qc_mentorship/)). IBM's Qiskit Advocate program needs its roughly $200 developer certification to move past the entry tier ([IBM blog](https://ibm.com/quantum/blog/qiskit-advocate-program); [certification](https://www.ibm.com/training/certification/ibm-certified-quantum-computation-using-qiskit-v2x-developer-associate-C9008400)). **MIT iQuHACK 2026 drew more than 1,400 participants from 76 countries**, most of them online ([The Tech](https://thetech.com/2026/02/19/iquhack-2026)), and QHack 2026 had a $67,000 prize pool ([QHack](https://qhack.ai/)). Qiskit itself says people can contribute "with just a few basic web development skills" ([Qiskit on X](https://x.com/qiskit/status/1521853593686614017)). A VR visualiser of Euler-rotation toy universes is a strong microgrant pitch, and it is the one project where her two interests become one portfolio.

That leaves about 8 minutes for re-runs, 4,000-shot versions and one full IBM tutorial, such as the error-mitigated CHSH run at about 2 minutes ([IBM CHSH tutorial](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Qiskit/documentation/main/docs/tutorials/chsh-inequality.ipynb)). Good swaps for a second month are the Deutsch-Jozsa "one-look judge" (about 3.4 s; [arXiv 2109.07910](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.07910), search summary), factoring 15 with Shor's algorithm (about 3 s; [IBM Shor tutorial](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Qiskit/documentation/main/docs/tutorials/shors-algorithm.ipynb)) and a repetition code that catches a flipped bit (under 10 s; [IBM tutorial](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Qiskit/documentation/main/docs/tutorials/repetition-codes.ipynb)). Too big for a free month: IBM's error-mitigated QAOA (22 minutes), spin-chain VQE (37 minutes) and Krylov (70 minutes) tutorials ([QAOA](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Qiskit/documentation/main/docs/tutorials/quantum-approximate-optimization-algorithm.ipynb); [VQE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Qiskit/documentation/main/docs/tutorials/spin-chain-vqe.ipynb); [Krylov](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Qiskit/documentation/main/docs/tutorials/krylov-quantum-diagonalization.ipynb)), and the 120-qubit GHZ record ([arXiv 2510.09520](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.09520); search summary).

Two notes keep the list honest. A CHSH score above 2 shows the correlations are quantum, but with both qubits on one chip it does not *certify* the coin's randomness against a cheating device; the true sentence is that the coin comes from the same kind of qubits that just beat the classical limit. And the face frame sends only numbers to IBM, never video, and no recording goes into any repo.

## Conclusion

Astra's real goal changes which part of this research matters most. The thing she can build first is not a better face tracker or a bigger quantum computer but a **personal dataset**: hours of her own Unified Expressions values, marked with what was happening, which no lab has and no clone can exist without. Retrieval over that data is cruder than the neural reaction models, but it is explainable, deletable and at home in a framework-free mind, and reaction researchers still keep it as the baseline to beat, which makes it an honest starting point rather than a toy. The same recordings also answer the quantum question better than a quantum computer can. What would make AI Astra's small moods feel like Astra's is the shape of her own variation, her blink rhythm and her drift back to neutral, measured from her face, not drawn from a more exotic source of bits.

Across every question the pattern from the first version of this report holds: the hard parts already exist, and what is missing is translation between communities. Unified Expressions is settled, so the Face Codec translates it into words a mind can use instead of replacing it. VRChat's OSC is settled, so her OSC problem and OSC Sonar come down to seeing which app holds which port, and letting only one app listen on it. IBM's hardware is free, so the Quantum 10 is about turning real runs into things people can see in VR, and the people who will use 2029-era machines for physics are the ones writing toy-universe circuits on today's free hardware. Each next step is small and in her hands: fixing the OSC overlap with OSCQuery or a router, one clone session in a private instance, and an Open Plan sign-up. Iris, the agent who will use the copy, comes after the copy exists. What has not happened yet, and matters more than any of the research, is the first live run.
