    More details
    How it works
    Press Start the show and play something on Spotify on this PC. The device boxes at the top say which part of the song each device follows: the kick and bass, the bass line, vocals and melody, snares and claps, hi-hats and cymbals, every beat, the tempo or the song’s sections. Song structure gives each device its job (the wall line the hard hits, snares and kick; Squiggle, the mic and the headset the vocals; the keyboard the hats in ripples, the mouse pad an even metronome, the Mirror the sections) and each part its own colour family. Synesthesia colours each part by the notes it hears, in rainbow order through the scale (C red, D orange, E yellow, F green, G cyan, A blue, B violet, the sharps between) and paler as the melody sits higher, after the pitch-class synesthesia studies Paper Girl’s chromesthesia quiz cites (Itoh et al. 2017; Ward, Huckstep and Tsakanikos 2006). Patterns with the song lets the wall line and the keyboard change pattern with the music’s pace: calm stretches roll slowly, steady ones flash on the hits, loud ones and drops snake, comet and chase. The room shares a few colours each section, colours hold longer when it’s calm, now and then the whole room goes one colour, and a rainbow runs through every light at a drop. The APC64’s pads show the bars. Pick a preset or change any setting: it goes to the lights straight away and is saved on the PC. In Pulse every light sits dark (or at Base) and flashes on its own part’s hits; White patterns does it in white, to learn the patterns first. The live view shows what the server hears: the song’s tempo in the dot, its sections, drops and accents, the five parts (a part’s bar lights up on its hits), the line from the lowest to the highest notes, and what the show is doing now (the pace, the strip’s and the keyboard’s patterns, the room’s colours, the Squiggle and the APC64).
    Where it comes from
    Light Hive’s server on this PC (findastra/rgbee server/, light-hive-server 1.5.0 and later). It hears Windows’ loopback of the default speakers (Voicemeeter Input on this PC, where Spotify plays), splits the sound into bands and finds the beats. Nothing is recorded or saved. The APC64’s pads are lit through its DAW port (the protocol from github.com/esazhin/apc64-control, not Akai’s); the server hands the APC64 back to Ableton whenever the show stops. Only your PC’s copy of this page reaches it; the live site only previews.
    Your rules
    2026-11-27: every beat changes the colour, parts of the line answer different parts of the sound, the line is the wave length, lots of settings to experiment with, solid blocks; dim lighting with bright flashes on the beats, one colour across larger sections, plain white to learn the patterns; more patterns and longer runs, sometimes only one colour; colours that slow down in some stretches and fit together; no bars on the keyboard (they’re on the APC64 now); snakes, rainbows and gradual rolls; the mouse pad just the tempo; more than one colour on the Squiggle. Fast strobing is on by your choice: you lifted Light Hive’s flash limit for your own room on 2026-11-27 (it was three a second, then six), so the lights can change every 40 ms. This page’s own preview changes about eight times a second at most, and a lit bar stays on at least 170 ms, and nothing on it moves when your computer asks for less motion.
    Not built yet
    The server doesn’t start by itself at sign-in, and the show doesn’t come back on after a restart (your settings do). Music playing on another device can’t be heard: pick this PC in Spotify Connect. MSI’s lights aren’t reached yet.
