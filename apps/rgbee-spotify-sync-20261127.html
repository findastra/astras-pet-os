More details
How it works
Press Start the show and play something on Spotify on this PC. The table at the top says which light follows which part of the song: the kick and bass, the bass line, vocals and melody, snares and claps, hi-hats and cymbals, every beat, or the tempo. Pick a preset or change any setting: it goes to the lights straight away and is saved on the PC. In Pulse every light sits dark (or at Base) and flashes on its own part’s hits; White patterns does it in white, to learn the patterns first. The live view shows what the server hears: the song’s tempo in the dot, its sections, drops and accents, the five parts (a part’s bar lights up on its hits), and the line from the lowest to the highest notes.
Where it comes from
Light Hive’s server on this PC (findastra/rgbee server/, light-hive-server 1.5.0 and later). It hears Windows’ loopback of the default speakers (Voicemeeter Input on this PC, where Spotify plays), splits the sound into bands and finds the beats. Nothing is recorded or saved. Only your PC’s copy of this page reaches it; the live site only previews.
Your rules
2026-11-27: every beat changes the colour, parts of the line answer different parts of the sound, the line is the wave length, lots of settings to experiment with, solid blocks; dim lighting with bright flashes on the beats, one colour across larger sections, plain white to learn the patterns. Fast strobing is on by your choice: you lifted Light Hive’s flash limit for your own room on 2026-11-27 (it was three a second, then six), so the lights can change every 40 ms. This page’s own preview changes about four times a second at most, and nothing on it moves when your computer asks for less motion.
Not built yet
The server doesn’t start by itself at sign-in, and the show doesn’t come back on after a restart (your settings do). Music playing on another device can’t be heard: pick this PC in Spotify Connect. MSI’s lights aren’t reached yet.
