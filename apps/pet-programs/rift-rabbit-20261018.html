1. VRChat on Quest Pro

Face and eye tracking through VRCFaceTracking.

Face tracking: VRCFaceTracking on
1.1 Native VRChat tracking

no head tracker

The headset tracks itself; nothing corrects it.

Starts: SteamVR → the streamer → VRCFaceTracking → VRChat

Turn off: Space Calibrator continuous calibration, Space Override, Space Sync

The baseline to compare the others with. Lighthouse trackers only line up if they were calibrated once; with no head tracker nothing pulls them back when the headset drifts.

1.2 Space Calibrator

head tracker, 2 positions for the head: the mounted tracker and the headset

The headset keeps its own position; continuous calibration keeps re-measuring the gap between it and the tracker.

Starts: SteamVR → the streamer → VRCFaceTracking → Space Calibrator → VRChat

Turn off: Space Override, Space Sync

What you use today. The two systems stay separate, so the gap it chases can grow during a long session.

1.3 Space Override

head tracker, 1 position for the head: the mounted tracker

After one calibration the head position is built from the tracker; everything is on the Lighthouses.

Starts: SteamVR → the streamer → VRCFaceTracking → Space Override → VRChat

Turn off: Space Calibrator continuous calibration, Space Sync

Not confirmed with Meta Horizon Link (cable or Air Link) yet.

Nothing can drift apart, but a head tracker that loses the base stations moves your view. It has a fallback to the headset if the tracker dies. Controllers can jump and settle; full-body in VRChat can look odd.

1.4 Space Sync

head tracker, 1 position for the head: the headset in charge

The headset keeps its own position; the tracker measures the gap and moves the Lighthouse devices to follow it.

Starts: SteamVR → the streamer → VRCFaceTracking → Space Sync (Follow SLAM HMD mode) → VRChat

Turn off: Space Calibrator continuous calibration, Space Override

Not confirmed with Meta Horizon Link (cable or Air Link) yet.

A beta fork of Space Override, tested mostly on one setup. Trackers can sway on fast head turns until the head is still.
